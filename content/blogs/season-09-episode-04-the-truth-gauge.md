The team compares screen claims, independent sensors, and physical signs. The trouble looked small at first, which made it easier for Cybertropolis to misunderstand.

Shadow stood with the team and watched the room choose between hurry and care. Byte began writing down what was known, what was guessed, and what still needed a witness.

### The Drop

BL4CK4T's mark appeared beside the first clue.

`THE SCREEN SAID NORMAL. THE FLOOR SAID RUN.`

Nobody treated the line as an answer. It was a handle, and the team had learned enough seasons to know that a handle still had to be tested.

### The Lesson Begins

The Truth Gauge came out in the middle of the room. It made the invisible parts visible enough for everyone to argue with the same picture.

Pixel leaned over the edge. "So the scary part is smaller than it sounds?"

"No," Jinx said. "It is more specific than it sounds."

That helped. Specific problems could be named, mapped, and answered. Vague fear only made the room louder.

### Trial And Error

The first plan moved too fast. The second plan named too little. The third plan finally gave people something useful to do: report what they saw, protect the people waiting, and stop guessing past the evidence.

The False Engineer wanted the city to look at the wrong part of the problem. The team kept bringing the work back to independent sensing, manual checks, and safety-first response.

### Closing Scene

By evening, the room sounded different. There were still questions, but they were honest questions, placed beside records and careful labels.

The next clue waits in Foundry Quarter.
