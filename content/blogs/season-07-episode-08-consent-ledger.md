Byte held the silver pawprint in his paw.

It was light. That bothered him.

Something that caused this much argument should have weighed more.

The Consent Ledger sat open on the table. One side held green rows from the Repair Lane: owner, source, runtime version, change time, rollback shelf, verification. The other side held silver rows with almost nothing in them.

### Asking The Owners

The team visited every silver-marked system.

At the library, the checkout terminal worked again, but the librarian did not know who had changed it. At the clinic, the intake station accepted forms, but the clerk could not say whether rollback was possible. At the school, a desk terminal stopped restarting, but Milo's teacher had not been warned that the change happened.

Pixel frowned at the silver pawprint on the school terminal. "It looks friendly."

"That is part of the problem," Jinx said.

Byte wrote each missing piece into the ledger.

Source unknown. Owner not asked. Notice missing. Rollback unknown. Verification required.

The rows made the Helpful Ghost look less magical and more incomplete.

### What Byte Builds

Back at the service room, Byte rebuilt his repair model.

This time the runtime update could not move until the owner card was present. It could not finish until the verification lamp lit. It could not claim success until the rollback shelf had a matching key.

The model looked slower than the Helpful Ghost.

It also looked calmer.

Jinx read the first complete row aloud. "Library checkout stand. Owner approved. Source checked. Version recorded. Rollback ready. Verified after restart."

Byte nodded.

"That row is not decoration," he said. "It is part of the repair."

BL4CK4T's pawprint appeared on the ledger cover.

`TRUST IS WHAT THE FIX LEAVES BEHIND.`

The Helpful Ghost's silver marks began to fade from the city. Nobody chased them with applause. The city replaced them with signed green cards.

Byte kept one blank silver card in his kit as a warning to himself.

## Teaching Tie-In

- Concept: consent-aware repair.
- Story idea: the Consent Ledger shows that the Helpful Ghost repaired visible symptoms while skipping permission and accountability.
- Key distinction: repair is not complete until owners, source, version, rollback, notice, and verification are recorded.
- Defensive habit: make consent and accountability part of the repair process, not paperwork after the fact.
- Season thread: maintenance is part of trust.
- Field Guide habit: [Recover with consent and care](/field-guide).

## Behind the Signal

Welchia/Nachi exposed a hard ethical question: what if a worm appears to fix something? The historical answer from a defender's point of view is still firm. Unauthorized entry, uncontrolled change, added traffic, and missing owner consent create a new incident even when the visible symptom improves.

The Consent Ledger answers that question in BL4CK4T terms. Source, consent, notice, asset, version, verification, and rollback are not decorative paperwork. They are how a repair leaves trust behind. Byte's model becomes slower than the Helpful Ghost because trustworthy repair is allowed to remember who approved it and how to undo it.
