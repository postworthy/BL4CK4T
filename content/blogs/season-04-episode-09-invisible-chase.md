The corrected notice took up less space than the rumor, which felt unfair until Jinx reminded everyone that truth often needed fewer decorations.

The City Chronicle printed the correction in plain type:

`The Vanishing Caller case involved real harm, incomplete public claims, and one wrong accusation that records disproved. The First Bell Desk will now collect identity-related incident reports before names reach the Notice Wall.`

Lark signed it herself, under the headline and in full view of the wall.

### The Drop

BL4CK4T's final Season 4 card arrived under the First Bell Desk bell: `THE BEST CHASE ENDS BY CHANGING HOW THE NEXT CHASE BEGINS.`

Grimalkin rang the bell once, and no one ran. That had become one of the city's better sounds.

### The Lesson Begins

The Caller entered a repair path set by Ms. Vale, the Glass Bureau, and the affected students.

There were apologies, restrictions, and work: helping rebuild the cabinet process, writing a clear account of what happened, and attending every meeting where someone explained the impact.

No crowd got to watch, and Whiskers approved of that. "Consequences are not theater," she said.

Pixel added that to the Notice Wall, then moved it because Jinx said the quote needed context.

### Trial And Error

The team updated the First Bell binder with a new identity-claim report form. It asked for the source of the claim, the evidence state, and the possible harm if the claim was published.

Shadow wrote the last line slowly. He had spent the season learning that unseen things could still leave marks, and visible things could still lie.

Jinx placed the Chase Map in the drawer beside the Copy Map from Season 3 and the Threadboard notes from Season 2.

"Different cases," she said. "Same habit."

"Evidence before story," Shadow said, and Jinx nodded. "And people before symbols."

### Closing Scene

At sunset, repair hours, club meetings, a Chronicle correction, and a First Bell reporting card made the Notice Wall look almost ordinary again. Then Pixel found the envelope: pink, sealed, neat as a secret, and pinned where the monster label had been.

On the front, in looping letters, someone had written:

`For everybody who wants to be liked.`

Byte reached for it, but Cipher caught his sleeve. "We do not open mystery mail at sunset," she said.

BL4CK4T's tiny black pawprint appeared on the seal, and nobody touched it after that.

## Teaching Tie-In

- Concept: incident aftermath and public correction.
- Story idea: the city changes how identity claims are reported before names reach the wall.
- Key distinction: closing a chase should change the next response.
- Defensive habit: preserve evidence before story and people before symbols.
- Season thread: Season 4 closes the Caller case and opens the Season 5 pink-envelope clue.
