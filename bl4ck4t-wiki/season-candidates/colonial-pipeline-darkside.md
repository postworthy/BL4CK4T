# Candidate: Colonial Pipeline / DarkSide

## Executive Summary

Colonial Pipeline is a strong future-season candidate because it shows how a cyber incident can move from business systems into public anxiety and operational disruption. The story is about ransomware, critical infrastructure, business continuity, communication, and the way fear can become part of the incident.

## Why This Is Season-Worthy

- It is one of the most visible U.S. critical-infrastructure cyber incidents.
- It connects ransomware to supply pressure and public behavior.
- It teaches operational continuity, incident communication, extortion economics, identity security, and resilience.
- It would let BL4CK4T revisit Season 4's lesson about public fear while moving into infrastructure.

## Historical Scope

- Time period: 2021 incident and policy aftermath.
- Core event: ransomware affected Colonial Pipeline's business network, leading the company to halt pipeline operations.
- Impact: fuel supply concerns, public anxiety, government response, ransomware payment debate, and critical-infrastructure policy changes.
- Aftermath: greater attention to critical-infrastructure security, incident reporting, ransomware response, and operational resilience.

## Fit With BL4CK4T Continuity

- Builds on Whiskers' public-calming role from Season 4.
- Uses Grimalkin's response-lane skill and Shadow's physical observation of real-world effects.
- Connects Glass Bureau process to broader civic infrastructure.

## BL4CK4T Transformation Potential

Cybertropolis's delivery ribbon stops moving after scheduling ledgers freeze. The city still has supplies, but no one trusts the routing desk. Lines form, rumors spread, and the Script Kitties learn that public communication and continuity planning are part of cyber defense.

## Likely Character Leads

- Whiskers: handles calm communication under pressure.
- Grimalkin: coordinates continuity routes.
- Shadow: observes how digital disruption becomes street-level behavior.
- Jinx: separates facts, rumors, and public claims.

## Likely Villains Or Forces

- A ransomware crew equivalent could appear as a fictional extortion group.
- The stronger story force may be the collision between extortion, shutdown, and public fear.

## Likely Concepts And Lore Needed

- Critical infrastructure.
- Business continuity.
- Ransomware economics.
- Public communication during incidents.
- Identity and remote access hygiene.

## Possible Episode Arc

1. The delivery ribbon stops and the city assumes the worst.
2. The team learns that business ledgers, not pipes, are frozen.
3. Public lines and rumors become a second incident.
4. Grimalkin maps alternate supply routes.
5. Whiskers prepares a plain-language public update.
6. Jinx tracks what is known, claimed, and paid attention to.
7. The city debates speed, payment, and resilience.
8. Services restart through controlled continuity lanes.
9. Cybertropolis creates incident communication rules for infrastructure events.

## Risks And Handling Notes

- Risk: oversimplifying IT impact versus operational technology impact.
- Handling: preserve the distinction between business-system disruption and operational shutdown decisions.
- Risk: making public anxiety look foolish.
- Handling: show why people worry when infrastructure feels fragile, while still correcting rumor.
