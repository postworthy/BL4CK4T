# BL4CK4T Business Model

## Summary

BL4CK4T should operate as a trust-first story IP and learning business. The public blog is the canon and should remain clean, generous, beautiful, and ad-free. Revenue should come from products and learning experiences that deepen the world: artifacts, case files, card decks, classroom kits, cyber club resources, and BL4CK4T University training.

Core operating line:

> Public canon builds trust. Social spreads memory. Products deepen participation. Education scales usage. Enterprise funds maturity.

## Brand Promise

BL4CK4T makes cybersecurity approachable, memorable, and morally serious through story, play, and worldbuilding.

The emotional promise is: learn to protect the city without losing your curiosity.

The educational promise is: readers do not just learn cyber concepts; they learn habits of wise response.

The enterprise promise is: story-driven cybersecurity training people actually remember.

Enterprise and classroom products should preserve the two-layer model: Cybertropolis makes the lesson memorable, and standards alignment makes the product defensible for teachers, security leaders, compliance teams, and learning buyers. University modules should be able to state which BL4CK4T habit they teach and how that maps to NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-61, CISA CPGs, or CIS Controls.

## Revenue Ladder

- Free: blog episodes, season pages, selected art, newsletter, basic Field Guide.
- Low cost: stickers, posters, printable case files, wallpapers, character cards.
- Mid tier: physical case decks, field notebooks, classroom packs, cyber club kits, case board or playmat.
- Premium education: homeschool curriculum, library event kits, facilitator kits, summer camp materials.
- Enterprise: BL4CK4T University modules, tabletop decks, awareness campaigns, role-based tracks, workshops, licensing.

## Monetization Guardrails

- Do not add traditional display ads to the reading experience.
- Do not use fear-driven marketing or manipulative funnels.
- Do not make the kid-facing experience dependent on direct child data collection.
- Do not turn BL4CK4T into generic hacker merch.
- Do not let enterprise training reshape public canon into compliance copy.
- Paid products should deepen the world, help readers remember a principle, help adults teach the material, or let people practice a habit.

## Operating Rule

If a product feels like it was pulled from Cybertropolis, it belongs. If it feels like generic merch with a logo pasted on it, wait.
