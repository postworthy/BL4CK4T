# Season 4 Production Bible: The Invisible Chase

## Production Purpose

This bible controls Season 4 production from mission packets through final public blog posts. It keeps the season aligned around public hacker mythology, social engineering, trace evidence, proportionality, and Shadow's growth.

Season 4 should read as one complete chase story. Every episode should work as a standalone blog post while moving the Vanishing Caller investigation forward.

## Season Promise

Season 4 promises a chase that resists becoming a mob story. The public reader should feel the pull of a mystery, the danger of a simple nickname, and the relief of watching the team insist on evidence before judgment.

The reader should leave with:

- a story-level understanding of social engineering and identity confusion;
- respect for trace evidence and knowledge-state discipline;
- sympathy for victims without panic;
- a clearer sense that accountability and proportionality belong together;
- curiosity for Season 5's love-letter clue.

## Story Engine

The season engine is the friction between the city's desire for a monster and the team's need for proof.

The Vanishing Caller makes mistakes and causes harm. The city makes another mistake by turning the Caller into a symbol before the evidence is ready. The Script Kitties must solve both problems.

## Tone

Use:

- phone clicks, notice boards, maps, reflections, and route lights;
- chase tension built from uncertainty;
- media pressure without cartoonish reporters;
- Shadow's watchfulness and Jinx's discipline;
- BL4CK4T drops that ask for understanding before capture.

Avoid:

- operational social-engineering scripts;
- making the Caller glamorous;
- making the city cruel beyond repair;
- turning the season into a lecture about ethics;
- copying real names or real exploit details into public fiction.

## Core Canon

### The Vanishing Caller

The Caller is the Season 4 adversary identity. The Caller should remain human-scale: clever, evasive, afraid of being reduced to a monster, and responsible for real harm.

### Notice Wall

The Notice Wall is the visible rumor surface. It should show public pressure and story distortion.

### Chase Map

The Chase Map is Jinx and Shadow's evidence board. It should preserve known, likely, ruled-out, and unknown traces.

### Mirrorline Arcade

Mirrorline Arcade is the public trace location. It should create reflection and identity confusion without becoming a secret lair.

## Character Production Notes

- Shadow: primary lead. Growth from invisible watcher to trusted witness.
- Jinx: evidence lead. Keeps rumor, inference, and fact separate.
- Whiskers: leader. Holds the line on proportional response.
- Grimalkin: plans safe pursuit lanes and stops crowd-chase behavior.
- Cipher: cleans false patterns and explains identity evidence.
- Byte: handles voice-copy and signal artifacts at a non-operational level.
- Pixel: notices how stories change as they pass through public spaces.
- BL4CK4T: pushes the team toward understanding, not spectacle.

## Season 5 Cliffhanger

The finale should close the Caller incident first. Then a sealed pink envelope appears on the Notice Wall with a friendly line that invites everyone to open it. The clue points toward Season 5's love-letter social lure without naming the historical inspiration.

## Publication Voice Rules

- Keep public prose story-forward.
- Teach through action, evidence, and consequence.
- Use teaching tie-ins for concise lesson clarity.
- Keep real-world references out of public fiction.
- Avoid safety language that interrupts story mystique; omit operational details instead.
