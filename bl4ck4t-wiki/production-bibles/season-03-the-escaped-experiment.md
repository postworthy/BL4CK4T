# Season 3 Production Bible: The Escaped Experiment

This bible controls Season 3 production from mission packets through final public blog posts. It keeps the season aligned around propagation, containment, safe testing, response coordination, and Byte's growth.

Season 3 should read as one complete incident arc. Every episode should work as a standalone blog post while moving the Copycat Sprite response forward.

## Season Promise

Season 3 promises a contained but tense incident story: a toy copying experiment escapes its expected boundary, and the Script Kitties must learn that good builders prepare for failure before they press run.

The public reader should feel:

- curious about how connected systems magnify mistakes;
- attached to Byte as a young builder learning accountability;
- aware that response work is organized, human, and practical;
- satisfied that the season resolves through repair and changed practice;
- ready for Season 4's shift into public hacker mythology and pursuit.

## Story Engine

The season engine is the friction between "it worked" and "it was ready."

Byte can make the Copycat Sprite run. The season asks whether Byte can make it stop, explain what happened, protect affected users, clean up after it, and build better rules for next time.

## Tone

Use:

- bright lab tension;
- resource counters, process slots, trust paths, rooms, maps, bells, and restore slips;
- suspense built from copying, delay, and uncertainty;
- practical response work;
- Byte's maker pride under pressure;
- Grimalkin's calm coordination;
- consequences that feel concrete.

Avoid:

- narrator scolding;
- repeated warning labels in story prose;
- real worm replication instructions;
- making Byte reckless beyond repair;
- making caretakers look foolish;
- treating the Copycat Sprite as a human villain.

## Core Canon

### Copycat Sprite

The Copycat Sprite begins as a toy process inside a learning simulator after Season 2. It should remain fictional, visual, and story-level.

### Resource Counters

Resource Counters are visible capacity indicators for affected learning rooms and lab systems. They should show copy count, queue pressure, tool delay, fan strain, and room availability so the public reader understands that the incident causes resource pressure without needing real worm mechanics.

### Process Slots

Process Slots are the limited running spaces available to learning-room tasks. The Copycat Sprite becomes harmful when extra copies occupy slots that students and caretakers need for ordinary work.

### Trust Paths

Trust Paths are older approved connections between learning systems. They are the clear BL4CK4T-world equivalent for inherited trust relationships and explain how the incident moves beyond Byte's intended sandbox.

### The Old Stack

The Old Stack is retired learning infrastructure beneath the Ledger Lab. Its old paths let the incident become larger than one sandbox without contradicting Season 1's Hushline outcome.

### First Bell Desk

The First Bell Desk becomes the city's standing lab incident coordination point. It complements the Glass Bureau.

## Character Production Notes

- Byte: primary lead. Growth from clever builder to responsible engineer.
- Grimalkin: response coordinator. Growth through ownership maps and calm process.
- Pixel: bridge from Season 2. Keeps wonder alive but learns to stop and record.
- Jinx: evidence discipline from Season 2 prevents blame and panic.
- Whiskers: team lead who protects affected students and keeps repair first.
- Cipher: pattern cleaner who explains counts and removes false leads.
- Shadow: physical observer who finds timing, lights, and room-state clues.
