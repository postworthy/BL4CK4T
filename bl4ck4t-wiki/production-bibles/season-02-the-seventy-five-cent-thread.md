# Season 2 Production Bible: The Seventy-Five Cent Thread

## Production Purpose

This bible controls Season 2 production from mission packets through final public blog posts. It keeps the season aligned around evidence, investigation, and Jinx's growth before any public episode is published.

Season 2 should be fully developed, drafted, reviewed, revised, and sequenced before the first Season 2 post goes live. The season should read as one complete investigative arc, not as separate mystery-of-the-week articles.

- After all episodes pass review, publish all Season 2 episodes simultaneously unless the user explicitly requests a different release plan.

## Season Promise

Season 2 promises a detective story built around tiny evidence, shared systems, and the discipline of not jumping ahead of the record.

The public reader should feel:

- pulled forward by a clue that seems too small to matter;
- attached to Jinx as she learns to turn suspicion into proof;
- curious about logs, accounting records, and shared systems;
- aware that evidence has to be preserved before it can persuade anyone;
- eager for the next season's shift from investigation to escaped experiment.

## Story Engine

The season engine is the friction between suspicion and evidence.

Jinx knows the `-0.75` mismatch matters before she can prove why. The world around her has plausible reasons to ignore it: it is small, it crosses boundaries, and most accounting mismatches are ordinary. The season advances every time the team turns one more hunch into a record, one more record into a pattern, and one more pattern into a case others can trust.

The antagonist is not only a hidden actor. It is also noise, impatience, jurisdiction, stale trust, and the temptation to treat an exciting theory as proof.

## Tone

Use:

- neon detective mood;
- terminals, ledgers, print spools, clipped notes, and maps;
- late-night lab stillness;
- small clues with large implications;
- character-driven evidence building;
- institutional friction that feels real rather than foolish;
- suspense built from uncertainty, not chase-scene noise.

Avoid:

- narrator scolding;
- repeated safety reminders in public-facing prose;
- operational intrusion procedure;
- making logs feel like homework;
- flattening institutions into useless adults;
- making the adversary more interesting than the investigation;
- importing real historical names into public fiction.

The story should make defensive work feel sharp, patient, and human.

## Core Canon For The Season

### The `-0.75` Thread

The `-0.75` clue from the Season 1 finale is the season opener. It should appear first as a model anomaly, then as a real Ledger Lab resource mismatch. It is not proof by itself. It is the first thread.

### The Ledger Lab

The Ledger Lab tracks shared compute time, storage credits, lab reservations, project queues, and print resources for student projects. It should feel useful, busy, slightly old, and easy to underestimate.

### The Civic Learning Grid

The Civic Learning Grid connects school labs, library terminals, museum kiosks, public learning servers, and old research relays. It was built for collaboration. That trust is its strength and its weakness.

### The Threadboard

The Threadboard is Byte and Jinx's evidence wall. It records, orders, compares, and visualizes. It does not attack, trap, or retaliate. It makes evidence legible.

### The Borrowed Door

The Borrowed Door describes account misuse from the defender's point of view. Public prose may show odd timing, mismatched usage records, or an account appearing where it should not. It must not teach how to gain or use unauthorized access.

### The Glass Bureau

The Glass Bureau is the process-heavy civic institution the team must persuade. It should be slow because proof is incomplete and ownership is unclear, not because it is stupid.

### The Far Relay

The Far Relay is the wider adversary layer: a brokered channel where stolen project notes, old access passes, and system rumors move. It gives the season scale without turning the plot into a single mastermind story.

### The Ledgerjack

The Ledgerjack is optional as a named adversary. If used, keep the Ledgerjack indirect and broker-like. The character should not personally cause every event. The better season antagonist is the ecosystem of ignored anomalies, borrowed access, and markets for secrets.

## Character Production Notes

### Jinx

Jinx carries the season. Her first instinct is right, but her first case is not ready. Give her the discomfort of being unable to prove what she feels. Her growth is not becoming more suspicious; it is learning to make suspicion useful to others.

Season endpoint: Jinx can present what was known first, what changed, what was ruled out, what remains unknown, and what the evidence supports.

### Grimalkin

Grimalkin maps the possible paths and keeps alternative explanations alive. Use his planner instincts for threat models, timelines, jurisdiction maps, and "what else could explain this?" checks.

Season endpoint: Grimalkin becomes a disciplined strategist who can hold worst-case thinking without letting it dominate the case.

### Byte

Byte builds the Threadboard and the season's defensive instruments. Keep his tools observational: collection, ordering, comparison, and display. His arc is about building tools that preserve truth rather than force a conclusion.

Season endpoint: Byte understands that a defensive tool's job is to make the record clearer.

### Shadow

Shadow notices quiet physical details: warm terminals, unclaimed print slips, status lights, odd kiosk behavior, and after-hours movement. His clues should be small but decisive.

Season endpoint: Shadow speaks up earlier when physical evidence connects to digital records.

### Whiskers

Whiskers keeps the case fair. He stops the team from accusing too early and from dismissing too quickly. He translates the team's findings for people outside the Hideout.

Season endpoint: Whiskers becomes a leader who protects the investigation's integrity.

### Pixel

Pixel gives curiosity and explanation energy without carrying the season. Use Pixel to ask why accounting records matter, why logs can tell stories, and why waiting can be active work.

Season endpoint: Pixel learns that curiosity sometimes means letting evidence accumulate before touching the shiny thing.

### Cipher

Cipher checks timing, sequence, repetition, and assumptions. Use her to separate pattern from coincidence and to show that messy records can still have structure.

Season endpoint: Cipher becomes sharper at cleaning assumptions before naming patterns.

### BL4CK4T

BL4CK4T drops should redirect method, not reveal answers.

Season drops:

- Opening: `SMALL DOES NOT MEAN EMPTY.`
- Early investigation: `A HUNCH IS A DOOR. EVIDENCE IS THE KEY.`
- Midpoint: `TRACE THE THREAD. DO NOT PULL THE CITY APART.`
- Institution friction: `MAKE THE RECORD SPEAK FOR ITSELF.`
- Finale: `SOME EXPERIMENTS DO NOT KNOW WHEN TO STOP.`

## Episode Requirements

### Episode 1: The Seventy-Five Cent Thread

Must accomplish:

- Open from the Season 1 `-0.75` clue.
- Move the anomaly into the Ledger Lab.
- Let Jinx notice and care before anyone else.
- Keep the clue small; no visible break-in or culprit yet.
- Use BL4CK4T drop `SMALL DOES NOT MEAN EMPTY.`

Must avoid:

- Starting with the adversary.
- Treating the mismatch as proof of a large plot.
- Explaining the entire Ledger Lab too early.

### Episode 2: The Ledger Lab

Must accomplish:

- Introduce shared resources, project accounts, queues, storage credits, and normal accounting noise.
- Let Byte map the system.
- Let Jinx learn normal before naming abnormal.
- Give Pixel a concrete "accounting is a sensor" insight.
- End with the mismatch repeating in a way that feels harder to dismiss.

### Episode 3: The Borrowed Door

Must accomplish:

- Show behavior behind the number.
- Use Shadow for a physical clue.
- Use Cipher for timing.
- Let Grimalkin name multiple hypotheses, including the Borrowed Door.
- Keep the explanation defender-facing and non-operational.

### Episode 4: The Threadboard

Must accomplish:

- Introduce the Threadboard.
- Show logs and records becoming a timeline.
- Let Byte's tool preserve evidence rather than force action.
- Put Whiskers and Jinx in productive tension about containment versus observation.
- End with a new external Grid point appearing on the board.

### Episode 5: Nobody Owns The Thread

Must accomplish:

- Introduce or elevate the Glass Bureau.
- Show the help desk and Bureau seeing the case differently from the team.
- Make institutional friction understandable.
- Let Jinx choose better evidence over louder accusation.
- Use BL4CK4T drop `MAKE THE RECORD SPEAK FOR ITSELF.`

### Episode 6: Old Trust Paths

Must accomplish:

- Expand beyond the Ledger Lab to libraries, kiosks, and research relays.
- Show old trust paths as useful but risky.
- Let Grimalkin's map become essential.
- Let Cipher remove at least one false pattern.
- Let Shadow confirm a physical-world detail.

### Episode 7: The Far Relay

Must accomplish:

- Reveal the wider market/broker layer.
- Introduce the Ledgerjack mark or rumor if using the adversary identity.
- Distinguish curiosity, intrusion, and selling secrets.
- Keep the adversary indirect.
- Use BL4CK4T drop `TRACE THE THREAD. DO NOT PULL THE CITY APART.`

### Episode 8: The Investigator's Case

Must accomplish:

- Let Jinx assemble the case clearly.
- Show what was known first, what changed, what was ruled out, and what remains unknown.
- Make the Threadboard legible to the Glass Bureau and Grid caretakers.
- Let Grimalkin show tested alternate theories.
- Let Byte demonstrate the evidence tool without turning it into a gimmick.

### Episode 9: What The Records Changed

Must accomplish:

- Resolve the Ledger Lab and Far Relay arc enough for the season to close.
- Show the city improving anomaly reporting and evidence preservation.
- Give Jinx a clear endpoint as disciplined investigator.
- Give each main Script Kitty a small payoff.
- Seed Season 3 with a toy process or experiment that begins copying itself too far.
- Use BL4CK4T drop `SOME EXPERIMENTS DO NOT KNOW WHEN TO STOP.`

## Season 3 Cliffhanger

The Season 2 finale should end after the investigative resolution. Let the team see the Ledger Lab's changes first: clearer records, shared reporting, and caretakers who now know how to preserve small anomalies.

Then introduce the Season 3 thread:

- Pixel runs a harmless toy process in a contained learning simulator.
- The process copies itself to help test the new evidence channel.
- It begins copying faster than expected or appearing in one extra sandbox.
- The team does not know whether it is a bug, a feature, or a mistake in the test.
- BL4CK4T drops: `SOME EXPERIMENTS DO NOT KNOW WHEN TO STOP.`

This points into the Morris Worm-inspired Season 3 without explaining the historical reference publicly.

## Season-Wide Continuity Rules

- The `-0.75` clue opens the season and should not be solved too quickly.
- Every major claim must move through evidence, not intuition.
- The Threadboard should grow visibly across episodes.
- The Glass Bureau should become more useful as evidence improves.
- The Far Relay should appear as a wider layer, not a single all-powerful villain.
- The Ledgerjack, if used, must remain a fictional broker, not a direct historical stand-in.
- Jinx should lead without becoming infallible.
- BL4CK4T should guide method, not reveal answers.
- Each episode should have a satisfying local mystery while building the season case.

## Publication Voice Rules

- Preserve the detective mystique of small clues and patient investigation.
- Let characters be clever, wrong, funny, frustrated, and specific.
- Avoid warning-label prose in public-facing story copy.
- Withhold operational detail through transformation and omission.
- Prefer concrete scene action over narrator explanation.
- Avoid overusing `lesson`, `safety`, `boundary`, `responsibility`, and similar process words in story prose.
- Run `TROPES.md` validation before any public-facing markdown is published.
