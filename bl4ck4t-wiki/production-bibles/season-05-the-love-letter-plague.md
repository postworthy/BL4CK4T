# Season 5 Production Bible: The Love Letter Plague

## Production Purpose

This bible controls Season 5 production from mission packets through final public blog posts. It keeps the season aligned around social lures, attachment trust, contact-list spread, warning culture, containment, and Jinx and Pixel's growth.

- Complete the Season 6 cliffhanger before publication.

## Season Promise

Season 5 promises a message incident that feels personal before it feels technical. The story should make readers understand why a kind-looking message can be dangerous, why warning people early matters, and why victims should not be shamed for trusting a familiar sender.

## Story Engine

The engine is the friction between affection and caution. The Glitter Letter wins when people want to believe the message. The team wins when they protect curiosity with process, warn without blame, and make reporting feel like care.

## Tone

Use:

- pink envelopes, glitter, mail trays, class lists, and routing lamps;
- suspense built from spread and uncertainty;
- warm friendship pressure;
- Jinx's evidence discipline under urgency;
- Pixel's curiosity learning containment.

Avoid:

- real malware behavior;
- attachment tricks that can be copied;
- shaming people who opened the letter;
- making Phishmonger more powerful than the incident evidence;
- lecture-heavy safety language in public prose.

## Core Canon

### Pink Envelope

Season 4's envelope opens the season but should be quarantined before normal handling.

### Glitter Letter

The Glitter Letter is the social-lure incident artifact. It spreads through trust paths in fictional terms.

### Quarantine Tray

The Quarantine Tray lets the team review suspicious messages without opening them in ordinary city paths.

## Character Production Notes

- Jinx: learns to warn early with clear uncertainty.
- Pixel: learns that curiosity needs containment.
- Whiskers: coordinates city warning without panic or shame.
- Cipher: explains appearance versus behavior.
- Byte: builds safe displays and spread models.
- Grimalkin: creates mail-handling lanes.
- Shadow: notices early recipient patterns.
