# Season 1 Production Bible: The Singing Network

## Production Purpose

This bible controls Season 1 production from mission packets through final public blog posts. It keeps all episodes aligned before anything is published.

Season 1 should be fully developed, drafted, reviewed, revised, and sequenced before the first Season 1 post goes live. The season should read as a complete run, not as isolated posts assembled one at a time.

## Release Strategy

- Do not publish any Season 1 episode until all Season 1 episodes are drafted and vetted.
- Complete the Season 1 finale and Season 2 cliffhanger before publication begins.
- After all episodes pass review, publish in planned order.
- Treat each episode as a standalone blog post that also advances the season.
- Keep the season arc private until the public posts reveal it through story.

Required before publication starts:

- All nine mission packets complete.
- All nine article drafts complete.
- Season continuity pass complete.
- Character arc pass complete.
- Technical/story concept pass complete.
- TROPES.md validation complete for every final public markdown file.
- `pnpm wiki:check` passes.
- `pnpm build` passes after public files are staged.

## Season Promise

Season 1 promises mystery, craft, and character growth built around an old city signal system that sings.

The public reader should feel:

- invited into a hidden layer of Cybertropolis;
- curious about how signals can carry meaning;
- attached to Pixel's wonder and the team's growing judgment;
- aware that tools, rumors, and public systems have consequences;
- eager for the next season's shift from signal mystery to investigative trail.

## Story Engine

The season engine is the friction between wonder and consequence.

The Echo Grid is beautiful because it makes Cybertropolis feel alive. It is dangerous because the city once mixed messages and commands in the same stream. The Script Kitties are not trying to defeat a monster so much as understand an old design, survive the rumors around it, and help the city outgrow a flaw without killing the music.

## Tone

Use:

- neon mystery;
- tactile gadgets;
- rooftop listening;
- old-city infrastructure;
- zines, charms, chalk marks, and signal cabinets;
- character-driven technical discovery;
- consequences that matter without becoming grim.

Avoid:

- narrator scolding;
- repeated warnings to the reader;
- exploit procedure;
- flattened "bad hacker" caricatures;
- making the season feel like a classroom worksheet;
- overexplaining the historical inspiration inside public fiction.

The story should humanize technical curiosity. The art is in the characters, the hidden system, and the cost of getting carried away.

## Core Canon For The Season

### Signal Row

Signal Row is the season's main stage. It includes billboards, antenna towers, message kiosks, public alert horns, courier lights, old routing cabinets, repair ladders, roof access, and late-night neon.

### Echo Grid

The Echo Grid is the old signal layer beneath Signal Row. It carried ordinary civic messages and routing instructions in the same stream. It should feel half-infrastructure, half-urban legend.

Public description: the Echo Grid is an old city system that sometimes hears a song as a command.

### Hushline

The Hushline is the later replacement network that separates messages from commands. It is not introduced as a lecture. It should arrive as the elegant answer the team has been circling all season.

### Tonebox

The Tonebox is Byte and Pixel's simulator artifact. It makes the invisible rule visible. It should be enticing, physical, and cinematic, but never a recipe for real-world abuse.

### Crunch Charm

The Crunch Charm is the season's myth object. It gives the public something simple to obsess over while the real issue stays hidden in architecture.

### The Little Blue Pawprint

The Little Blue Pawprint is the street zine that spreads the Echo Grid rumor. It should feel handmade, exciting, incomplete, and a little dangerous because of what readers project onto it.

### Row Rebels

The Row Rebels are curious peers, not stock villains. They include tinkerers, status-seekers, idealists, pranksters, and kids who want the city to feel like it belongs to them.

### Signal Imp

The Signal Imp is optional. If used, they should be charismatic but not central enough to shrink the season into a single antagonist plot. The strongest antagonist remains rumor plus flawed infrastructure.

## Character Production Notes

### Pixel

Pixel carries the season's wonder. Give Pixel the first real emotional connection to the Echo Grid. Pixel should be wrong in interesting ways: too eager, too enchanted, too quick to touch the shiny thing. The growth is toward craft, not caution as a personality replacement.

Season endpoint: Pixel becomes a vivid explainer who can keep wonder alive without letting it become a dare.

### Cipher

Cipher turns tone into pattern. Use her to name rules, challenge assumptions, and show that old systems can be messier than clean puzzles. She should sometimes over-trust the beauty of a pattern, then learn to leave room for exceptions.

Season endpoint: Cipher becomes a systems thinker, not just a codebreaker.

### Byte

Byte makes the invisible physical. Use Byte for the Tonebox, models, diagrams, old cabinets, and the Hushline demonstration. Byte's arc is about craft: a good tool has intent, limits, and a clear story.

Season endpoint: Byte treats restraint as part of invention.

### Jinx

Jinx is the first to feel the cost of rumor. Use her for zine tracking, copycat behavior, false closure evidence, and the moment the team admits the Echo Grid has moved beyond mystery.

Season endpoint: Jinx becomes a disciplined investigator whose suspicion matures into proof.

### Shadow

Shadow notices small physical inconsistencies: late blinks, repeated kiosk text, old cabinet routing, timing mismatches, reflected pawprints. Give Shadow clues that seem small until the story needs them.

Season endpoint: Shadow speaks sooner when a detail matters.

### Whiskers

Whiskers holds the team together. He should not dampen curiosity; he should help the team carry it. His strongest moments are when he names the moral shape of the situation without sounding like a lecture.

Season endpoint: Whiskers becomes a steadier leader by listening before deciding.

### BL4CK4T

BL4CK4T drops should sharpen the story rather than explain it. Keep them short, memorable, and slightly mysterious.

Season drops:

- Opening: `LISTEN. MAP. STOP.`
- Early warning: `Not every song is meant to be answered.`
- Rumor turn: `A secret repeated without context becomes a dare.`
- Midpoint: `A discovered rule asks what kind of builder you are.`
- Finale: `A city should never mistake a song for an order.`

## Episode Requirements

### Episode 1: The Sound Beneath Signal Row

Must accomplish:

- Open with Signal Row at night.
- Pixel hears the tone first.
- Shadow notices a physical effect.
- Cipher identifies that the pattern has structure.
- BL4CK4T drops `LISTEN. MAP. STOP.`
- End with the Echo Grid named or strongly implied.

Must avoid:

- Explaining too much history.
- Turning the first episode into a full technical lesson.
- Letting the team solve anything major.

### Episode 2: The Listeners' Marks

Must accomplish:

- Reveal older marks and signal-listener culture.
- Give Pixel emotional kinship with past listeners.
- Give Jinx the first real discomfort.
- Let Shadow find a mark that is current, not old.
- End with the sense that someone else is listening now.

### Episode 3: The Little Blue Pawprint

Must accomplish:

- Introduce the street zine.
- Show rumor spreading through Packet Market and Signal Row.
- Make the zine exciting enough that the reader understands why it spreads.
- Let Jinx track the rumor's path.
- End with copycat behavior escalating.

### Episode 4: The Crunch Charm

Must accomplish:

- Introduce the Crunch Charm as a public obsession.
- Let Byte and Cipher dismantle the myth without killing the mystery.
- Introduce the Tonebox prototype.
- State the key idea in story language: the city mistakes some songs for commands.
- End with the charm myth still spreading despite the team's better explanation.

### Episode 5: The Row Rebels

Must accomplish:

- Introduce the Row Rebels as mixed motives, not villains.
- Let Pixel sympathize with them.
- Let Whiskers and Jinx see the civic stakes.
- Show at least one fake or misdirected public message.
- End with the sense that the Echo Grid has moved beyond the team's control.

### Episode 6: The Tonebox Demo

Must accomplish:

- Make the Tonebox feel captivating.
- Let Byte and Pixel enjoy the craft of building.
- Let Cipher keep the model honest.
- Let Whiskers question how public the demo should become.
- End with the Tonebox becoming known outside the team or copied in distorted form.

### Episode 7: The False Closure

Must accomplish:

- Use the false closure as the season's consequence turn.
- Keep the incident serious but not catastrophic.
- Let Jinx and Shadow solve the evidence trail.
- Let Whiskers choose containment over blame.
- Use the midpoint drop: `A discovered rule asks what kind of builder you are.`

### Episode 8: The Hushline

Must accomplish:

- Introduce or elevate the retired Signal Row maintainer.
- Show the Hushline as a better architecture.
- Let Cipher explain message versus command through the city model.
- Let Byte build the split-path demonstration.
- End with Signal Row changing, not simply being "fixed."

### Episode 9: The City Still Sings

Must accomplish:

- Resolve the Echo Grid arc.
- Build the listening exhibit.
- Give each main Script Kitty a small payoff.
- Include BL4CK4T's final Season 1 message.
- Seed Season 2 with a tiny resource/accounting discrepancy.

## Season 2 Cliffhanger

The Season 1 finale should end after the emotional resolution, not before it. Let the team enjoy the listening exhibit and the quieter Signal Row first.

Then introduce the Season 2 thread as a small, almost ignorable anomaly:

- A maintenance receipt shows a `0.75 credit` mismatch.
- A kiosk usage ledger is off by `0.75`.
- A tiny metering badge on the Hushline model blinks `-.75`.
- Jinx notices it after everyone else has moved on.

Recommended final beat:

Jinx stays behind after the listening exhibit closes. The new Hushline meter should read zero. It reads `-0.75`.

She taps the glass once.

The number does not change.

Across the darkened billboard, a BL4CK4T pawprint appears beside three words:

`FOLLOW SMALL THREADS.`

This points into Season 2's Cuckoo's Egg-inspired detective arc without explaining the historical reference publicly yet.

## Season-Wide Continuity Rules

- The Echo Grid starts as mystery, becomes rumor, creates consequence, then becomes architecture the team understands.
- The Hushline should not appear as the full answer too early.
- The Crunch Charm should remain a symbol, not the real cause.
- The Tonebox should be compelling, but its purpose is revelation, not control.
- The Row Rebels should evolve across episodes rather than appear once and vanish.
- Signal Row Maintenance Office should be imperfect but sympathetic.
- BL4CK4T should guide through pressure and questions, not exposition.
- Every episode should contain a satisfying local story even while building the larger arc.

## Publication Voice Rules

- Preserve the mystique of technical discovery.
- Let the characters be clever, funny, wrong, and specific.
- Avoid warning-label prose in public-facing story copy.
- Withhold operational detail through transformation and omission.
- Prefer concrete scene action over narrator explanation.
- Avoid overusing `lesson`, `safety`, `boundary`, `responsibility`, and similar process words in story prose.
- Run `TROPES.md` validation before any public-facing markdown is published.

## Production Workflow

1. Create mission packets for all nine episodes.
2. Review all mission packets as a set.
3. Draft all nine episodes in `bl4ck4t-wiki/drafts/`.
4. Run a full season continuity pass.
5. Run a character arc pass.
6. Run a Season 2 cliffhanger pass.
7. Run `pnpm wiki:check`.
8. Run `pnpm tropes:check bl4ck4t-wiki/drafts/<draft-file>.md` for each episode draft.
9. Revise all drafts as a complete season.
10. Only after approval, promote all nine episodes to `content/blogs/`.
11. Run `pnpm tropes:check` against public markdown.
12. Run `pnpm build`.
