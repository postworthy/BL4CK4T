# Season 6: The Day The City Would Not Answer

## Logline

Cybertropolis learns that a city can be harmed even when nothing is stolen, because services people depend on can stop answering.

## Historical Inspiration

Primary anchor: the 2007 Estonia cyberattacks.

Contextual background: early large-scale DDoS history, including Mafiaboy, may be used in the historical account to explain public recognition of denial-of-service harm. Estonia remains the season-scale anchor because it better supports civic dependency, public-service disruption, attribution uncertainty, and national resilience.

## BL4CK4T Transformation

Season 5 ends with empty delivery receipts flooding the Message Office. Season 6 turns that clue into an availability crisis: blank receipts, frozen kiosks, stalled civic forms, delayed public services, restricted outside gates, and honest status communication under uncertainty.

## Season Theme

- Technical theme: availability, DDoS, degraded service, resilience, dependency mapping.
- Character theme: Whiskers learns that honest uncertainty can be leadership.
- Ethical theme: a blocked door is harm when people need to enter.

## Character Wound

Whiskers is afraid that saying `we do not know yet` will make him look weak. The season should test his desire to answer quickly and mature him into a leader who can communicate uncertainty without guessing.

Key line to preserve if it fits the eventual story:

> A leader who guesses loudly can make a quiet crisis worse.

## Core Lessons

- Availability is part of trust.
- Nothing has to be stolen for real harm to occur.
- Public status communication is part of response.
- Critical services need triage, redundancy, and continuity plans.
- Attribution and motive may remain uncertain during response.

## Planned Story Shape

1. Cold open inside consequence: a clinic, school, or public service waiting while the system spins.
2. The team first searches for changed records and finds none.
3. Jinx begins mapping which systems are delayed, degraded, unavailable, or safely pausable.
4. Whiskers issues a status update that admits what is known and unknown.
5. Pixel learns that absence and silence can be evidence.
6. The Service Map reveals which services need priority protection.
7. The Flood Prince leaves a claim that nothing was stolen.
8. Whiskers reframes blocked access as real harm.
9. The city establishes rate limits, priority lanes, public status procedures, and fallback plans.
10. The season closes with one service on the Service Map marked red because it failed from old patch debt, not the flood.

## Planned Villain Shape

The Flood Prince represents attention without responsibility. He should not be written as a grand mastermind or a one-note prankster. His human detail is that interruption makes him feel seen. His moral failure is believing that wanting attention gives him permission to blind everyone else.
