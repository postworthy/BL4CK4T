# Season 8: The Aurora Vault

## Logline

After the loud Patch Bell War, Cybertropolis faces a quieter danger: someone is not breaking the city, but carefully copying the plans that protect its future.

## Historical Inspiration

Operation Aurora.

## Historical Account Requirement

- Required account path: `bl4ck4t-wiki/historical-accounts/operation-aurora.md`
- Status: not started.

## BL4CK4T Transformation

The Season 7 quiet log leads the Script Kitties into the Aurora Vault, where source scrolls, build records, trust keys, and city blueprints are kept. Nothing obvious is broken. The mystery is built from small impossibilities: a build stamp that does not match, a low-level key opening a high-level room, and a blueprint read so carefully that the shelf dust looks untouched.

## Season Theme

- Technical theme: targeted intrusion, quiet persistence, access control, crown-jewel protection, strategic theft.
- Character theme: Jinx learns that absence, timing, and tiny impossibilities can be evidence; Grimalkin learns old protections must be renewed.
- Ethical theme: copying can cause harm when the copied thing protects tomorrow.

## Character Wounds

Jinx trusts evidence, but struggles when the evidence is almost invisible.

Grimalkin believes the old vault rules are still enough.

Key line to preserve if it fits the eventual story:

> Some thieves do not steal the jewel. They copy the map to the jewel.

## Core Civic Artifact

Crown Jewel Register: a classification system for the city's most important assets, especially assets whose exposure would endanger future defenses.

## Core Lessons

- Some thefts are quiet because the original remains.
- Crown jewels need different protection than ordinary records.
- Access that looks legitimate can still be wrong in context.
- Sensitive public communication may need to protect the investigation without misleading the city.
- Old controls should be reviewed against new threats.

## Planned Story Shape

1. A build stamp does not match its source record.
2. A low-level key opens a high-level room.
3. The team finds evidence of reading, not obvious breaking.
4. The Crown Jewel Register is introduced.
5. The team learns that not every asset deserves the same protection.
6. The Glass Dragon uses mirrored access to look like a trusted maintainer.
7. Pixel struggles because nothing appears to have happened.
8. Grimalkin realizes the old vault rules are not enough.
9. The team rotates keys, reduces access, and builds better logging.
10. A partial villain-view episode may show choices and philosophy without operational detail.
11. The Glass Dragon escapes with part of a blueprint.
12. The season closes when the stolen blueprint points to an old physical machine beneath the city.

## Planned Villain Shape

The Glass Dragon represents quiet theft and strategic deception. He believes copying is harmless because the original remains. The story should refute that without making him theatrical or omniscient.
