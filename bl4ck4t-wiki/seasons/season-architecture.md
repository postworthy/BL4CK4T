# Season Architecture

## Summary

BL4CK4T stories should develop like a streaming series. The existing three posts are the pilot season. Later seasons follow major cybersecurity eras or campaigns, each transformed into a safe, fictionalized Cybertropolis arc.

## Season Model

- Pilot season: establishes BL4CK4T drops, the team, and lesson-driven storytelling.
- Historical seasons: each season uses one major real-world campaign or era as inspiration.
- Public posts: each episode is still a standalone blog article with a clear lesson.
- Wiki role: season pages maintain continuity, theme, character arcs, villains, source requirements, and safety boundaries.

## Historical Grounding Requirement

Historically inspired seasons must not move directly from a headline or summary into BL4CK4T fiction. Each historical season first needs a sourced historical account under `bl4ck4t-wiki/historical-accounts/`.

The historical account must establish:

- What happened.
- When it happened.
- Who the key actors and institutions were.
- What technical systems or vulnerabilities mattered.
- What is known, uncertain, disputed, or inferred.
- What consequences followed.
- Which sources support each major claim.
- Which claims are known, alleged, inferred, disputed, or unknown.
- What people knew at each stage versus what later analysis revealed.
- What changed afterward in law, policy, defensive practice, culture, or public imagination.

Only after that account is complete should the season translate the event into BL4CK4T villains, districts, artifacts, missions, and public episodes.

## Macro Series Arc

The series should escalate historically and emotionally:

1. Hidden rules can be discovered.
2. Tiny clues can reveal invisible systems.
3. Connected systems make mistakes bigger.
4. Technical power creates public fear and responsibility.
5. Human trust can be exploited.
6. Digital society needs resilience.
7. Code can affect the physical world.
8. Cyber conflict can spill past borders.
9. Trust layers can be attacked.
10. Small observations can protect global systems.

## Production Rule

Season pages can be planned from creative direction. Historically inspired season development requires a completed historical account before transformation work. Episode drafts require source pages, mission packets, continuity review, and safety review before publication.

## Cross-Season Continuity Rule

Every season after the pilot must be reviewed against prior seasons before publication. The review must check:

- prior season endings and cliffhangers;
- recurring character growth;
- recurring locations, artifacts, factions, and villains;
- unresolved threads;
- whether new material weakens or contradicts prior season lessons.

Use [Cross-Season Continuity Standard](../canon/cross-season-continuity-standard.md) and update [Continuity Timeline](../canon/continuity-timeline.md) during the review.
