# Season 10: The Army Of Little Eyes

## Logline

Cybertropolis learns that forgotten everyday devices can become someone else's army without their owners meaning harm.

## Historical Inspiration

Mirai and the 2016 Dyn DDoS attack.

## Historical Account Requirement

- Required account path: `bl4ck4t-wiki/historical-accounts/mirai-dyn-ddos.md`
- Status: not started.
- No BL4CK4T transformation or episode drafting should proceed until the account is complete.

## BL4CK4T Transformation

The tiny devices Pixel notices at the end of Season 9 become the Season 10 center: toy owls, classroom clocks, weather buttons, door cameras, vending machines, lights, monitors, and old routers. They are not villains. Their owners are not attackers. But many still use factory names and default keys, and someone has called them into a flood.

## Season Theme

- Technical theme: IoT botnets, default credentials, device inventory, owner education, manufacturer responsibility.
- Character theme: Pixel learns that wonder matures into caretaking.
- Ethical theme: forgotten is not the same as free.

## Character Wound

Pixel loves little gadgets and does not want to see them as dangerous. The season should not make him cynical. It should teach that loving a connected thing includes helping it belong safely.

Key line to preserve if it fits the eventual story:

> The little eyes were not wicked. They were waiting for someone to teach them better names.

## Core Civic Artifact

Device Census: a citywide inventory of connected things, their owners, update status, names, and safe retirement paths.

## Core Lessons

- What you forget may still be answering someone else.
- Owners, builders, sellers, and the city all share responsibility.
- Default names and keys can create public risk.
- Counting devices is the first step toward caring for them.
- Response should educate innocent owners rather than shame them.

## Planned Story Shape

1. A new wave of outages begins.
2. The traffic comes from ordinary homes, classrooms, shops, and forgotten closets.
3. Citizens become defensive because their devices are being called attackers.
4. Pixel refuses to treat the devices as villains.
5. Jinx creates the Device Census.
6. The team discovers repeated factory names and default keys.
7. The Nameplate Factory is introduced.
8. The Shepherd of Little Eyes calls thousands of forgotten devices.
9. The team must stop the flood without punishing innocent owners.
10. Byte argues that manufacturers must ship safer defaults.
11. The city creates the Caretaker's Checklist.
12. Pixel helps a younger child rename a toy and give it a safer key.
13. The season closes by tracing repeated factory names to a shared update supplier, setting up a future supply-chain macro arc.

## Planned Villain Shape

The Shepherd of Little Eyes represents exploiting neglected responsibility. He does not own the army; he calls it. His moral failure is treating other people's forgotten things as if they belong to him.
