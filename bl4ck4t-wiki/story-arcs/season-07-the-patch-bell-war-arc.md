# Season 7: The Patch Bell War Arc

## Import Summary

Season 7 transforms the MSBlaster/Welchia documentary spine into a Cybertropolis maintenance crisis around the City Runtime, a common operating layer used by desk terminals, service-room machines, public boards, clinic intake stations, library checkout stands, school work surfaces, and permit counters. The red Service Map mark from Season 6 leads first to the visible Civic Pages District, but the team discovers that the known repair belongs to a wider runtime used across ordinary workstations. The city builds a trusted Repair Lane, then faces the Helpful Ghost, a silver unauthorized repair force that fixes some symptoms while breaking consent.

## Documentary-To-World Map

| Historical movement | BL4CK4T transformation |
| --- | --- |
| The warning before the fever | The red Service Map mark reveals an old known City Runtime repair notice. |
| Exposed doors in the worm era | Public boards and service-room machines running the same outdated runtime are reachable enough for symptoms to appear across the city. |
| Blaster turns delay into disruption | Desk terminals restart, public boards repaint, support queues fill, and ordinary work is interrupted. |
| Ordinary institutions feel cost | Students lose drafts, permit counters pause, clinic intake stations fall back to paper, and library checkout terminals loop. |
| Repair channel problem | The Script Kitties build the Repair Lane for verified, signed City Runtime fixes. |
| Helpful worm moral complication | Helpful Ghost pushes unsigned runtime repairs without consent and leaves no ledger trail. |
| What changed | The city adopts the Patch Bell rule: patch, isolate, or risk-accept openly. |

## Season Shape

1. The red mark from Season 6 points to a known unrepaired City Runtime notice.
2. The Red Clerk explains that the visible boards still worked and an old runtime update once broke public services.
3. Symptoms appear first on public boards because those systems face the city.
4. The team discovers the same runtime is installed on desk terminals and service-room machines.
5. The harm becomes ordinary: lost drafts, restarting terminals, stale notices, full support queues, and confused citizens.
6. Cipher hesitates because no option is perfectly safe.
7. Byte builds repair tools and initially admires the Helpful Ghost's effectiveness.
8. Jinx insists that repair must have a source, signature, and ledger trail.
9. The city rejects both silent delay and forced repair.
10. The Patch Bell becomes durable practice.
11. A quiet pre-fever access log shows someone read source plans before the loud crisis, opening Season 8.

## Character Arcs

- Cipher learns that responsible defenders sometimes act under bounded evidence rather than waiting for impossible certainty.
- Byte learns that a clever fix without authorization is still a violation.
- Jinx keeps the repair process honest by demanding source, signature, and consent.
- Whiskers helps the city explain why patching is not private housekeeping when public services depend on it.
- Grimalkin makes rollback and risk acceptance visible.
- Pixel sees that a terminal that "usually works" can still be carrying risk.
- Shadow notices when public symptoms point back to service-room behavior.

## Season 8 Bridge

After the Patch Bell is adopted, Shadow and Jinx find a quiet access log from before the Red Door fever. Someone read source plans from a protected room and left almost no disturbance. The next season should treat this as a quiet-intrusion bridge, not as another worm.
