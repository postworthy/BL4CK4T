# Season 3 Story Arc: The Escaped Experiment

## Adaptation Purpose

Season 3 transforms the Morris Worm historical spine into a BL4CK4T story about Byte, Pixel, Grimalkin, and the rest of the Script Kitties learning that connected systems magnify mistakes.

The season is not about an evil mastermind. It is about an experiment that leaves its intended boundary, a builder who has to own the consequences, and a team that learns response discipline.

## Historical Spine To Preserve

- A young network trusts old paths.
- A skilled builder makes an experiment that can copy itself.
- The experiment spreads farther than expected.
- Harm appears as resource pressure and stalled work, not destroyed files.
- Early responders do not understand the full shape at once.
- Coordination matters because every affected site sees only part of the problem.
- Accountability matters even when intent was not destruction.
- The aftermath changes response practice.

## Core Story Engine

The season advances every time the team discovers that the problem is larger than one screen but smaller than panic. Each episode should turn one visible symptom into one better response practice.

## Imported Infrastructure

- [Resource Counters](../concepts/resource-counters.md): visible BL4CK4T-world counters for copy count, queue pressure, tool delay, fan strain, and room capacity. They translate Morris Worm resource exhaustion into clear story pressure without operational mechanics.
- [Process Slots](../concepts/process-slots.md): limited places where learning-room tasks can run. Extra Copycat Sprite copies occupy these slots, so the harm is delay, crowding, and stalled work rather than destroyed records.
- [Trust Paths](../concepts/trust-paths.md): older approved connections between learning systems. They explain how the Copycat Sprite reaches beyond Byte's intended sandbox without forcing the story through vague tunnels or unrelated city metaphors.

## Character Arcs

- Byte: starts proud that the toy process works, then learns that a builder must design stops, logs, limits, and cleanup before the demo runs.
- Grimalkin: moves from planner to incident coordinator, assigning owners, timing updates, and keeping the team out of each other's way.
- Pixel: carries the spark from Season 2 and learns that curiosity can help discover a problem without becoming its excuse.
- Jinx: applies Season 2 evidence discipline to keep the response factual.
- Whiskers: protects the team from blame theater and keeps the response focused on repair.
- Cipher: separates pattern from noise and helps describe the spread without teaching it.
- Shadow: finds physical and timing clues in rooms where counters do not tell the whole story.

## Episode Arc

1. `The Ninth Marker` - The Season 2 toy process produces one extra marker and occupies one unexpected process slot. Byte proposes a cleaner sandbox test.
2. `The Sandbox Door` - Byte builds the Copycat Sprite test and documents expected count, pace, process-slot limits, stop controls, and cleanup limits.
3. `More Than Once` - The sprite appears in extra rooms through an old trust path, and the team sees the first boundary failure.
4. `Slow Rooms` - Classrooms and lab queues stall as extra copies occupy process slots and push resource counters upward.
5. `The Copy Map` - Jinx and Cipher prove spread through timestamps, copy counts, room states, process slots, and trust-path records.
6. `Grimalkin's Bell` - Grimalkin starts a response desk and assigns containment lanes.
7. `The Builder's Note` - Byte admits the test design missed failure controls.
8. `Clearing The Rooms` - The team coordinates cleanup, recovery, and caretaker updates.
9. `The First Bell` - The city adopts standing experiment rules, and a Season 4 chase clue appears.

## Season 4 Bridge

After the First Bell Desk opens, an anonymous caller leaves a taunting message about being blamed before being understood. The clue should point toward public hacker mythology, social engineering, pursuit, and proportionality without naming the Mitnick/Shimomura historical anchor.

## Public Story Constraints

- Do not describe real worm replication paths.
- Do not name the Morris Worm or real institutions in public fiction.
- Keep the Copycat Sprite fictional, visual, and contained in story metaphor.
- Let consequences, downtime, and repair carry the ethical weight.
