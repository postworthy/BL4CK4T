# Season 4 Story Arc: The Invisible Chase

## Import Summary

Season 4 transforms the hacker-manhunt documentary structure into a Cybertropolis story about an anonymous caller, a public nickname, trace evidence, social engineering, and the danger of letting fear write the ending before the facts arrive.

The season keeps the Mitnick/Shimomura-inspired spine at the level of story function: public hacker mythology, pursuit, technical traces, defender uncertainty, media amplification, legal/process friction, and proportionality. It does not use real names, real victims, or operational techniques in public fiction.

## Core Promise

The Script Kitties must find the Vanishing Caller without becoming part of the chase myth. Shadow learns that hiding and protecting are different choices. Jinx learns to keep evidence clear even when the public wants a monster.

## Historical Movement Mapping

| Documentary movement | BL4CK4T-world movement |
| --- | --- |
| The name before the evidence | The Notice Wall names the Caller before the team has proof. |
| A researcher's systems are touched | The First Bell Desk receives calls that repeat private phrases. |
| The chase becomes a story | The City Chronicle turns the Caller into a public figure. |
| Capture is not closure | The team finds the Caller but must still account for harm, motive, and proportional response. |
| Free Kevin and the counter-story | Whiskers refuses both hero worship and panic punishment. |
| What the chase left behind | Cybertropolis adopts a better way to report identity and trace claims. |

## Season Engine

The engine is the pressure between speed and proof. Each episode gives the city a faster story and gives the Script Kitties a harder job: slow the claim, preserve the trace, protect people, and respond in proportion.

## Character Arc

- Shadow: starts by valuing invisibility as protection, then learns that trust sometimes requires being seen by the right people at the right time.
- Jinx: keeps the evidence standard high when rumors become emotionally satisfying.
- Whiskers: protects the team from public pressure and keeps the final response proportional.
- Grimalkin: turns chase energy into planned lanes and known decision points.
- Cipher: cleans false patterns from the map.
- Byte: tracks how voice-copy rumors move without building dangerous tools.
- Pixel: notices how stories change as they pass between people.
- BL4CK4T: appears through drops that push the team toward understanding before capture.

## Episode Arc

1. `The Monster Word` - The Season 3 caller becomes a public rumor when the Notice Wall repeats the line.
2. `Notice Wall` - Jinx classifies claims while the City Chronicle prints the first dramatic nickname.
3. `Shadow's Trace` - Shadow follows physical route clues through Mirrorline Arcade.
4. `The Borrowed Voice` - The team sees how identity can be imitated without teaching a script.
5. `Mirrorline Arcade` - A public chase creates more noise than evidence.
6. `The Chase Map` - Jinx and Shadow build a map of confirmed, likely, ruled out, and unknown paths.
7. `The Wrong Poster` - The city almost blames the wrong student because the story is easier than the proof.
8. `Understanding First` - The team reaches the Caller and chooses containment, evidence, and accountability over spectacle.
9. `The Invisible Chase` - Season closes with a corrected public record, proportionate response, and a Season 5 clue: a love-letter styled note that should not be opened.

## Continuity Requirements

- Pick up the exact Season 3 anonymous caller thread.
- Keep the First Bell Desk active but distinct from the Glass Bureau.
- Do not make BL4CK4T solve the case.
- Do not make the Caller responsible for Ledgerjack, the Echo Grid, or Copycat Sprite.
- Do not portray the City Chronicle as malicious.

## Season 5 Cliffhanger

After Season 4 closes, a sealed pink envelope arrives at the Notice Wall addressed to `everybody who wants to be liked`. It should point toward the ILOVEYOU-inspired Season 5 without naming the real-world event in public fiction.
