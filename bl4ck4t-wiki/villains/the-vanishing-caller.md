# The Vanishing Caller

## Summary

The Vanishing Caller is the Season 4 adversary identity opened by the anonymous Season 3 phone call. The Caller turns fear into cover by letting Cybertropolis argue about a name instead of the evidence.

## Public Motif

- Payphone clicks.
- Empty receiver hooks.
- Notice-wall silhouettes.
- Borrowed voices repeated almost correctly.

## Personality

The Caller is clever, theatrical, defensive, and hungry to be understood on their own terms. The character should feel young enough to make poor choices and skilled enough to create real consequences.

## Inspiration Class

Public hacker mythology, social engineering, pursuit narratives, and disputed proportionality in 1990s hacker-manhunt stories.

## Safe Tactic Abstraction

Public stories show defender-facing effects: misleading calls, rumors, incomplete traces, copied language, and pressure on institutions. Do not include scripts, impersonation playbooks, or operational pursuit methods.

## Defensive Lesson Mapping

- Verify identity through context and records.
- Treat rumors as leads, not proof.
- Preserve trace evidence.
- Match response to evidence and harm.
- Keep accountability separate from spectacle.

## Episode Hooks

- A call repeats a private phrase from the First Bell Desk.
- The City Chronicle prints a nickname before the team has a timeline.
- A chase map points to empty rooms and borrowed voices.

## Published Canon Details

- Season 3 ends with an anonymous caller saying, `If they chase me, they will have to understand me first.`
- Season 4 should reveal the Caller through evidence and choices, not through a theatrical confession.
