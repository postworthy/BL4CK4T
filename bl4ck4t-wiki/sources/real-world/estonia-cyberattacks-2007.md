## Purpose

Source page for Season 6 historical research: the 2007 cyberattacks against Estonia, the Bronze Soldier crisis, DDoS and service availability, attribution uncertainty, civic digital dependence, national cyber resilience, and NATO policy aftermath.

This page is a source index only. It is not a BL4CK4T-world adaptation document.

## Extraction Notes

- The event should be anchored in Estonia's specific political and historical context around the Bronze Soldier relocation, not treated as a generic outage story.
- The strongest technical center is availability disruption: DDoS/DoS, website and e-mail disruption, DNS pressure, banking accessibility, and public-facing service degradation.
- Attribution must be written carefully. Sources support political linkage to the Estonia-Russia conflict and Russian-language mobilization; they do not support an unqualified statement that the Russian state directly executed the attacks.
- The defender story is central: Estonia's mitigation, temporary foreign-traffic filtering, NATO expert assistance, incident coordination, and later strategic/legal/organizational changes.
- The primary defender story should now draw from CERT/RIA: domestic traffic was prioritized; foreign access to government sites was restricted and restored gradually; CERT coordinated response with Estonian experts, Finnish CERT, foreign providers, and other national CERTs.
- The civic story is central: Estonia's high reliance on e-services and online banking made degraded availability socially and politically meaningful.
- Damage estimates should be caveated. Contemporaneous reporting includes large claims, but methodology and categories require careful explanation.
- Do not include attack instructions from historical sources in public-facing fiction. Use only high-level mechanism, consequences, response, uncertainty, and lessons.
