# BL4CK4T Visual Style Guide

## Purpose

This guide extracts visual canon and style direction from the current BL4CK4T image assets. Use it when commissioning, generating, or revising art for characters, season posters, stickers, thumbnails, lesson images, and worldbuilding references.

## Core Look

BL4CK4T art uses a dark cyberpunk city with bright instructional signage. The world is built from deep teal shadows, cyan glow, hot magenta accents, lime-green eyes, wet pavement reflections, dense high-rise silhouettes, rooftop signs, old terminals, data boards, warning panels, and hand-drawn cyber motifs.

Characters are stylized anthropomorphic cats with:

- charcoal, black, gray, or cream fur;
- large pointed ears with dark inner-ear linework;
- oversized expressive eyes that glow or catch neon;
- small pink or muted rose noses;
- simplified but sharp muzzle shapes;
- hoodie-based identity colors;
- black pants or dark practical lower layers;
- thick black outlines and graphic shading;
- serious, capable expressions even when the scene is playful.

The overall finish sits between animated-series poster art, trading-card character design, sticker-ready logo work, and neon comic illustration.

## Palette

Primary environment colors:

- near-black blue, ink black, dark charcoal;
- deep teal and cyan for city glow;
- hot magenta and electric pink for warnings, social-lure motifs, and poster contrast;
- lime green for BL4CK4T eyes, Whiskers, cyber status lights, and the core cat-logo eye language;
- purple for Cipher, mystery, crypto, and night skyline depth;
- orange for Pixel, curiosity, energy, and warm learning moments.

Avoid beige, cream, brown, or soft pastel dominance. Warm colors work best as accent identity colors against a dark teal/cyan/magenta city.

## Line And Rendering

- Use bold black outlines around characters.
- Use thick graphic folds on hoodies and sleeves.
- Keep faces clean and readable from sticker distance.
- Let neon rim light outline ears, shoulders, and hoodie edges.
- Use glowing signage for exposition instead of text boxes whenever possible.
- Keep backgrounds dense but organized: signs, windows, wires, symbols, and skyline layers should frame the story without swallowing the characters.
- Posters can be visually busy; character cards and logos should stay centered and readable.

## BL4CK4T Logo

Source: [BL4CK4T.png](../assets/BL4CK4T.png).

The logo is a hooded black cat bust, cropped at mid-torso, with a large dark-gray hood and black cat ears visible behind it. The face is mostly black negative space with luminous yellow-green eyes, a small green nose, and a thin cyan accent along the mouth or whisker line. White highlights trace the hood folds and facial contours.

The hoodie includes cable-like drawstrings and small device-cord details, which make the logo feel cybernetic without becoming cluttered. Around the figure are broken circular arc marks, dark navy to black, speckled with tiny star-like dots. The tail curls on the left. The background is white, making the sticker silhouette clear.

Logo rules:

- Preserve the hooded black-cat silhouette.
- Preserve the neon green eyes.
- Preserve the partial circular orbit marks when using the full logo.
- Keep the face mysterious and mostly shadowed.
- Do not add a visible full face, mouth detail, or human-like features.
- For stickers, use a clean white or transparent background and strong outline separation.

## Character Card Format

Sources: seven `*-Character.jpeg` files.

Each card uses:

- portrait orientation;
- teal, foggy city silhouettes in the background;
- upper-body character pose above a warm parchment-like information card;
- bold black all-caps name;
- thick horizontal divider lines;
- blocky, poster-like typography;
- role, hoodie color, specialty, and short character text;
- arms-crossed pose for most characters;
- centered frontal composition.

The character-card format is useful for reference sheets, but future polished cards should correct text overflow and typos before public use.

## Team Composition

Sources: [script-kitties-crowd.jpeg](../assets/script-kitties-crowd.jpeg), [season1.png](../assets/season1.png), [season2.png](../assets/season2.png), [season3.png](../assets/season3.png), [season4.png](../assets/season4.png), [season5.png](../assets/season5.png).

The team works best as a lineup in front of dense Cybertropolis signage:

- Pixel in orange often sits toward the left.
- Byte in blue/cyan stands near Pixel.
- Jinx in magenta stands left-of-center.
- Whiskers in bright green anchors the center.
- Shadow in cyan/teal stands right-of-center.
- Grimalkin in olive green stands near Shadow.
- Cipher in purple often crouches or stands at the far right.

This lineup gives a readable color rhythm: orange, blue, magenta, green, teal, olive, purple.

The team should not look uniformed. Their shared style is hoodies, dark pants, city lighting, and focused expressions; their individuality comes from color, pose, and tool language.

## Cybertropolis Visual Language

Cybertropolis is a vertical neon city packed with cats, signs, windows, cables, screens, rooftop silhouettes, and damp reflective streets. It feels dense and alive rather than empty or sterile.

Recurring city elements:

- glowing signs labeled `DATA`, `TECH`, `SCRIPT KITTIES`, `LEDGER LAB`, `THREADBOARD`, `SANDBOX`, `QUARANTINE`, `NOTICE WALL`, `FIRST BELL`, `PINK ENVELOPE`;
- high-rise canyons with cyan and magenta windows;
- neon waveform lines, dotted route lines, thread lines, and pulse paths crossing the sky;
- cats perched on rooftops, balconies, signs, and fire escapes;
- wet pavement reflecting teal, blue, and pink light;
- city-scale lesson boards that turn abstract cybersecurity ideas into visible civic infrastructure.

Use signs and diagrams as part of the world, not as separate explanatory overlays.

## Season Poster System

The Season 1-5 posters define a reusable format:

- top title: distressed cyan `SCRIPT KITTIES` with cat-ear shapes;
- small `PRESENTS`;
- central distressed white `BL4CK4T`;
- season number and season title in colored text;
- short thematic phrase beneath;
- dense Cybertropolis background with season-specific signs;
- team lineup across the lower third;
- BL4CK4T cat icon near the bottom center;
- bottom location/tagline text.

Season-specific visual motifs:

- Season 1: Signal Row, waveform lines, old signal signage, live-feed/data boards.
- Season 2: Ledger Lab, account logs, `-0.75`, Threadboard, receipts, balance boards, tiny mismatch graphics.
- Season 3: Sandbox, Copy Map, quarantine warning, recovery signs, pixel-sprite propagation motifs.
- Season 4: Notice Wall, rumor posters, Chase Map, Wrong Poster, First Bell, caution tape, route tracing.
- Season 5: pink envelopes, quarantine tray, address-book bloom, report/warn signage, contact-spread map.

Future season posters should keep this format but replace the sign language with the new season's artifacts and lessons.

## Lesson Image Style

### Shared Secret

Source: [shared-secret.png](../assets/shared-secret.png).

Two Script Kitties, Pixel in orange and Byte in cyan-blue, stand on a rooftop or high ledge against a purple night skyline. They stir glowing paint or liquid vats: warm yellow/orange, cyan/blue, and purple. The image explains cryptographic mixing through physical color blending. The mood is serious, collaborative, and hands-on.

Use this approach when visualizing abstract technical concepts: turn the concept into a tactile object that glows in the city.

### Compound Interest

Source: [compound-interest.png](../assets/compound-interest.png).

Pixel wears the orange hoodie and performs an energetic dab beneath neon arithmetic `67+5` and a bright upward arrow on a purple brick wall. The image is playful, high-contrast, and poster-simple. It pairs a math idea with a meme-like pose without losing the character design.

Use this approach for lighter educational moments: one character, one clear equation or diagram, strong neon line art, and a simple dark background.

### ClassOverride

Source: [classoverride.png](../assets/classoverride.png).

This image is a logo/terminal title card: green pixel text on a dark scanline background with a green pawprint. It feels like a CRT terminal, command-line lesson, or retro hacker-lab interface.

Use this approach for tools, projects, labs, or terminal-based learning references. It should stay cleaner and more minimal than story posters.

## Typography

Use three typography modes:

- distressed brush/graffiti cyan for `SCRIPT KITTIES`;
- distressed block white for `BL4CK4T`;
- clean neon sans or terminal text for signs, lesson boards, and project cards.

Avoid polished corporate fonts. Type should feel like city signage, old terminals, sticker graphics, or student-made cyber posters.

## Prompting Guidance For Future Generated Art

Include:

- "stylized animated cyberpunk cats";
- "dark teal neon city";
- "cyan and magenta signage";
- "thick black outlines";
- "hoodie-color identity";
- "wet reflective pavement";
- "dense Cybertropolis background";
- "glowing cybersecurity lesson signs";
- "serious but youthful expressions";
- "poster art / trading-card illustration".

Avoid:

- realistic fur rendering that fights the graphic style;
- generic hacker hoodies without feline identity;
- beige or daylight palettes;
- empty backgrounds;
- excessive photorealism;
- weapons, intimidation poses, or criminal glamour;
- UI text that cannot be read or that includes real exploit commands.
