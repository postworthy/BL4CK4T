# Real World Transformation Guide

## Summary

Real cybersecurity material can feed BL4CK4T stories, but it should be transformed into a fictional, playful, educational form.

The transformation should preserve intrigue. Remove exploit-enabling details quietly; do not replace them with repeated narrator warnings. The goal is not to flatten the hacker mystique, but to channel it into character, craft, consequence, and curiosity.

## Transformation Pattern

- Real APT behavior becomes a fictional villain pattern.
- Real tooling becomes a fictional city artifact, scanner, lock, signal, or puzzle.
- Real TTPs become high-level story mechanics, mysteries, and consequences.
- Real companies become fictional city institutions unless the public lesson needs a named historical reference.
- Real bugs become fictional weak points with clear story stakes.
- Real incidents become stylized missions with operational detail withheld or transformed.

## Mapping Guidance

Internal pages can record inspiration categories such as phishing, supply chain compromise, credential abuse, ransomware, botnets, espionage, or destructive attacks. Public stories should use fictional names and focus on what defenders learn.
