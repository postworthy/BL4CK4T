# Desk Terminals

## Summary

Desk terminals are the ordinary work computers of Cybertropolis. Students write reports on them, clinic clerks enter forms through them, permit offices process requests with them, libraries check out materials on them, and caretakers use them to review service status.

They are not magical boards or metaphor-only devices. They are recognizable workstations inside the BL4CK4T world.

## Story Function

- Provide a direct fictional equivalent for ordinary computers affected by worm-era patch failures.
- Let public stories show restarts, lost unsaved work, support queues, emergency updates, and recovery without forcing those effects onto signs or kiosks.
- Keep technical cause and human consequence close together.

## Visual Motifs

- Worn desk screens with stickered corners.
- Save lamps, restart chimes, and paper fallback drawers.
- Classroom rows, clinic counters, permit windows, library checkout desks, and caretaker benches.

## BL4CK4T Teaching Frame

Desk terminals make endpoint security concrete. A vulnerability or bad update does not harm an abstract machine; it interrupts people doing school, clinic, library, government, or maintenance work.

## Story Hooks

- A student loses unsaved work when a desk terminal restarts.
- A clinic intake station falls back to paper because the terminal cannot hold a form.
- Public Terminals helps local owners count affected workstations before a repair batch.

## Canon Notes

- Some desk terminals run the City Runtime.
- Public Terminals helps coordinate shared machines, but local owners still understand local impact.
- A terminal can restart, freeze, lose unsaved work, or require a verified update without showing exploit mechanics.
