# Mission: The Flood Prince

## Summary

A crown-marked claim appears: nothing was stolen, so nothing was harmed. Cipher and Jinx prevent the city from treating a claim as proof, while Whiskers answers the moral argument: wanting attention does not give anyone permission to blind everyone else.

## Canon Status

Draft canon until the public Season 6 release is approved.

## Historical Documentary Anchor

- Movement 5: attribution uncertainty, claims, political messages, denials, and incomplete proof.

## Featured Characters

- Cipher: keeps claim, proof, inference, rumor, and motive in separate buckets.
- Jinx: checks whether the crown mark explains evidence or only tries to own the story.
- Whiskers: rejects the villain's narrow definition of harm.

## Location

- Status Wall, with the Service Map nearby to show actual impact.

## Villain Or Conflict

- [Flood Prince](../villains/flood-prince.md): attention without responsibility. He claims interruption is harmless because nothing was stolen.

## Opening Control

Open with the claim arriving before any proof supports it. Do not open with the team discovering a new technical symptom.

## Story Beats

1. A flood crown mark appears with a taunt about harmless knocking.
2. Citizens repeat the claim because it sounds like an explanation.
3. Cipher refuses to let the mark become attribution by itself.
4. Jinx compares the claim to the Service Map's actual waiting people.
5. Whiskers says the blocked-door moral line.
6. The team keeps investigating while responding, rather than pausing response for perfect attribution.

## Lesson Payload

- Concept: [Status Communication](../concepts/status-communication.md).
- Defensive takeaway: claims and symbols are evidence to evaluate, not proof by themselves.
- Teacher note: use this episode to model careful language.

## Teaching Tie-In Target

- Concept: Attribution discipline.
- Story idea: the Flood Prince claims the interruption, but the team still checks evidence.
- Key distinction: a claim is not proof.
- Defensive habit: separate what is known, alleged, inferred, and unknown.
- Season thread: availability is part of trust.

## Safety Constraints
