# Mission: Nobody Owns The Thread

## Summary

The team brings the early case to the help desk and the Glass Bureau. The response is slow because the evidence crosses school, library, museum, and relay ownership. Jinx chooses better evidence over louder accusation.

## Canon Status

Draft canon until the Season 2 episode set is approved.

## Featured Characters

- Jinx: feels dismissed and learns to make the record clearer.
- Whiskers: translates the case into institutional language.
- Grimalkin: maps ownership and escalation paths.
- BL4CK4T: drops `MAKE THE RECORD SPEAK FOR ITSELF.`

## Location

- Ledger Lab help counter.
- Glass Bureau intake room.

## Villain Or Conflict

The conflict is institutional uncertainty, not stupidity. No single office owns the whole thread yet.

## Lesson Payload

- Concept: [Incident Reporting](../concepts/incident-reporting.md).
- Defensive takeaway: reports need context, ownership, and evidence.
- Teacher note: show why acting without proof can create harm.

## Real-World Inspiration

- Early computer intrusion cases where new problems crossed old jurisdictions.

## Public Transformation Notes

- Historical bureaucratic disbelief becomes understandable civic process friction.
- The story respects caretakers and responders.

## Safety Constraints

- Avoid portraying reckless accusation as heroic.
- Keep the team focused on records they can explain.

## Episode Hook

The Glass Bureau gives the team a queue ticket so transparent Jinx can read the Threadboard through it.
