# Mission: Priority Lanes

## Summary

The team builds priority lanes and fallback desks for essential services. Byte wants to make the cleverest routing model, Grimalkin keeps the work practical, and Whiskers learns that prioritization must be explained or it will look like favoritism.

## Canon Status

Draft canon until the public Season 6 release is approved.

## Historical Documentary Anchor

- Movement 4: defender triage under uncertainty.
- Movement 6: resilience practices after the event.

## Featured Characters

- Grimalkin: guides triage rules and fallback desks.
- Byte: builds fictional pressure models and learns that response tools must serve people, not elegance.
- Whiskers: communicates why some lanes move first.

## Location

- Queue District fallback desk and Status Wall priority notices.

## Villain Or Conflict

- The conflict is hard prioritization. The Flood Prince's pressure forces the city to choose what must answer first.

## Opening Control

Open inside a manual fallback desk already helping one person. Do not open with the team planning the desk.

## Story Beats

1. A fallback clerk handles one clinic form by hand while the normal board lags.
2. Byte proposes a model that ranks services but misses a human edge case.
3. Grimalkin defines priority lanes: urgent, essential, delayed, and safely paused.
4. Whiskers posts a public explanation before rumors frame priority as unfairness.
5. A non-critical service volunteers to pause so a critical service can answer.
6. BL4CK4T's drop asks whether speed is fair if nobody explains the line.

## Lesson Payload

- Concept: [Civic Resilience](../concepts/civic-resilience.md).
- Defensive takeaway: incident response needs priority rules and fallback paths before everything is urgent.
- Teacher note: show tradeoffs without making the team cold.

## Teaching Tie-In Target

- Concept: Triage and continuity planning.
- Story idea: the team keeps essential services moving through priority lanes and fallback desks.
- Key distinction: prioritizing is not ignoring everyone else.
- Defensive habit: define critical services and fallback procedures before a crisis.
- Season thread: availability is part of trust.
