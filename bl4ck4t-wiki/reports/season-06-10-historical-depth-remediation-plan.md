# Season 6-10 Historical Depth Remediation Plan

## Why This Exists

The first Season 6-10 release failed because the story layer moved faster than the historical layer. Earlier seasons worked better because they had enough nonfiction mass: source lists, evidence classification, chronology, ambiguity, defender perspective, cultural context, technical mechanism, aftermath, documentary movements, and only then fictional import.

The current Season 6-10 macro arc is useful as direction, but it is not a substitute for historical understanding. It must not be used as the basis for public episodes until each season has a historical account and documentary treatment with depth comparable to Seasons 1 and 2.

## Depth Benchmark

Use these earlier artifacts as the quality floor:

- Season 1 historical account: source list, claim tables, myth correction, technical background, enforcement, damage caveats, and scope recommendation.
- Season 1 documentary treatment: strong cold open, seven nonfiction movements, recurring motifs, interview/archive targets, narration guardrails, and open research needs.
- Season 2 historical and documentary artifacts: deep timeline, investigator perspective, institutional disbelief, technical mechanism, and Cold War context.

Line count is not the goal. It is a warning light. A short historical account for a major campaign is probably too thin unless the subject is narrow and the source record is limited.

## Rebuild Rule

Rebuild one season at a time. Do not draft public-facing fiction for a season until that season has:

- Source page in `bl4ck4t-wiki/sources/real-world/`.
- Historical account marked `research-foundation-complete`.
- Documentary treatment marked `complete`.
- Historical depth review recorded in this report or the season draft review.
- Story-world import based on the completed documentary treatment, not only on the macro arc.

## Required Historical Account Depth

Each Season 6-10 historical account must include:

- At least eight high-value sources, preferring primary or near-primary material.
- A source list that explains why each source matters.
- Evidence classification separating known, alleged, inferred, disputed, and unknown claims.
- A chronology that preserves what was known at the time.
- Technical background that explains the mechanism without becoming a how-to.
- Infrastructure background that treats the system as a character in the history.
- Attacker story and defender story.
- Victim and institutional specificity.
- Attribution discussion with confidence levels and alternative interpretations.
- Damage or impact estimates with methodology caveats.
- Legal, policy, technical, cultural, and practice aftermath.
- A source-backed claim table.
- Open questions that remain unresolved.

## Required Documentary Treatment Depth

Each documentary treatment must include:

- A cold open that begins with a concrete contradiction, image, sound, or human consequence.
- A documentary thesis that is more specific than "this event was important."
- At least six movements unless the historical account justifies fewer.
- For each movement: historical function, narrative question, key beats, evidence anchors, visual/audio language, and uncertainty guardrails.
- Recurring motifs that can later become fictional artifacts without distorting history.
- Interview targets or archival voices.
- Narration guardrails stating what can be said plainly, what must be qualified, and what must be avoided.
- An ending focused on what changed.

## Season-Specific Research Targets

### Season 6: Estonia 2007

- What happened between the Bronze Soldier dispute and the cyberattacks?
- Which services were affected, when, and how?
- What did Estonian institutions know during the first days versus later analysis?
- How did DDoS, website defacement, botnets, online mobilization, and public messaging differ?
- What is known, alleged, and disputed about Russian state involvement, patriotic hackers, and attribution?
- How did Estonia, NATO, CERT/incident-response bodies, banks, media, and citizens respond?
- What changed afterward in national cyber defense and NATO cyber thinking?

### Season 7: Early-2000s Worms, Patch Debt, And Unauthorized Repair

- Which events belong in the season spine: Code Red, Nimda, SQL Slammer, MSBlaster, and Welchia/Nachi?
- What vulnerabilities and patch timelines mattered without turning the account into exploit instruction?
- How did internet speed, exposed services, monoculture, patch lag, and user confusion combine?
- How did MSBlaster affect ordinary users and organizations?
- Why was Welchia/Nachi morally complicated despite trying to remove Blaster and patch systems?
- What changed in patch management, scanning, emergency response, and vendor communication?

### Season 8: Operation Aurora

- What was publicly disclosed by Google in January 2010?
- Which organizations were reported affected, and which details remain disputed?
- What did investigators and companies say about source-code access, Gmail account targeting, and intellectual property?
- How should attribution to China or China-linked actors be worded?
- How did browser exploitation, targeted intrusion, persistence, and crown-jewel access fit together at a high level?
- What changed in corporate security, public attribution, and technology-company posture toward nation-state intrusion?

### Season 9: Stuxnet

- What was discovered in 2010, by whom, and in what order?
- What did researchers establish about Windows systems, Siemens Step7, PLC logic, centrifuge manipulation, and stealth?
- Which claims about U.S./Israeli involvement are sourced, alleged, widely believed, or still officially unconfirmed?
- How did the malware manipulate physical systems while presenting misleading information?
- What impact on Iranian centrifuges is known or estimated, and what remains disputed?
- What changed in cyber-physical security, doctrine, and public imagination?

### Season 10: Mirai And Dyn

- How did Mirai recruit insecure IoT devices at a high level?
- What role did default credentials, consumer devices, manufacturers, and poor maintenance play?
- What happened to Dyn in October 2016, and which services were affected?
- How did the Mirai source-code release change the threat landscape?
- What is known about the original authors, their motives, and later prosecutions?
- What changed in thinking about IoT security, device accountability, and availability?

## Rebuild Sequence

1. Season 6 source page.
2. Season 6 historical account.
3. Season 6 documentary treatment.
4. Season 6 historical depth review.
5. Season 6 BL4CK4T import and support wiki pages.
6. Season 6 production bible.
7. Season 6 mission packets and drafts.
8. Season 6 quality gate, continuity audit, historical-anchor analysis, TROPES check, build, commit, and push.
9. Repeat the same path for Seasons 7, 8, 9, and 10.

## Non-Negotiables

- Do not batch-draft Seasons 6-10 from the macro arc.
- Do not use a lightweight historical account as a story anchor.
- Do not move to fictional import while major chronology, attribution, defender, victim, or aftermath gaps remain open.
- Do not publish if the documentary treatment cannot produce distinct episode movements.
