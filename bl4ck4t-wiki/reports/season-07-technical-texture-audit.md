# Season 7 Technical Texture Audit

## Purpose

This audit checks the rebuilt Season 7 public posts against the [Technical Texture Standard](../style-guides/technical-texture-standard.md). It does not revise the MSBlaster/Welchia historical account. The question is whether the public fiction preserves the historical shape: a known patch gap, exposed services, common vulnerable systems, operational disruption, verified repair, emergency change, rollback, unauthorized repair, consent, and durable patch-management practice.

## Summary Judgment

Pass. The rebuilt season removes the strained prior Episode 4 model and the vague cabinet-language that supported it. It introduces the City Runtime, desk terminals, and service rooms as explicit BL4CK4T-world infrastructure, then uses those elements to preserve the MSBlaster/Welchia-era lessons without relying on confusing metaphor.

The season now has a clearer technical line:

- a known City Runtime repair exists before the crisis;
- public-facing runtime systems show the first visible symptoms;
- ordinary desk terminals restart and interrupt work;
- service rooms provide evidence, support queues, update staging, and rollback context;
- the Repair Lane provides trusted updates;
- the Helpful Ghost demonstrates unauthorized repair;
- the Consent Ledger and Patch Bell make ownership durable.

## Residual Risk

Low. Public fiction still compresses many real organizations and technical processes into Cybertropolis institutions, but the core analogy is now much easier to follow because the world contains recognizable endpoints, backend service rooms, and a common runtime layer.
