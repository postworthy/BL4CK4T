# Series Continuity Audit: Pilot Through Season 2

## Scope

This audit establishes the first cross-season continuity baseline for BL4CK4T. It reviews the public pilot season, the published Season 1 arc, and the private Season 2 draft set.

Continuity status by season:

- Pilot season: public hard continuity.
- Season 1: public hard continuity.
- Season 2: private draft continuity until approved and published.

## Result

No blocking contradictions were found.

Season 2 correctly follows the Season 1 cliffhanger, keeps the Hushline outcome intact, develops Jinx without erasing earlier team roles, and points toward Season 3 without prematurely replacing the Season 2 resolution.

## Cross-Season Checks

| Check | Result | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prior season ending preserved | Pass | Season 1 ends with the Hushline working and the `-0.75` mismatch appearing. Season 2 opens from that exact state. |
| Prior cliffhanger honored | Pass | Season 2 uses `-0.75` as its first thread and moves it from Hushline model to Ledger Lab records. |
| Prior lesson preserved | Pass | Season 1's lesson about separating messages and commands remains solved for Signal Row. Season 2 does not reopen Echo Grid as the same problem. |
| Character growth consistent | Pass | Jinx's Season 2 lead role grows naturally from her Season 1 risk-spotting and false-closure evidence work. |
| BL4CK4T role consistent | Pass | BL4CK4T remains unseen and guides method through drops rather than solving the case. |
| Recurring location consistency | Pass | The Hideout remains the team's base. Signal Row remains Season 1's primary district. Ledger Lab is a new Season 2 location connected through the Civic Learning Grid. |
| Recurring artifact consistency | Pass | Hushline remains a Season 1 repair/architecture concept. The Threadboard is a new Season 2 evidence object. |
| Villain and adversary consistency | Pass | Row Rebels remain mixed-motive kids, not retroactive villains. Ledgerjack remains indirect and broker-like. |
| Open thread continuity | Pass | The Season 2 toy-process cliffhanger creates a clear Season 3 thread without reopening Season 1 or Season 2 conclusions. |
| Duplicate concept risk | Pass | Echo Grid/Hushline and Ledger Lab/Threadboard teach different concepts: signal architecture versus evidence/accounting. |

## Character Continuity

### Jinx

Pilot and Season 1 establish Jinx as the uncomfortable-question asker and risk spotter. Season 1 Episode 7 already gives Jinx evidence work around the false closure. Season 2 makes that thread central and develops her into a disciplined investigator. No contradiction found.

### Whiskers

Whiskers remains the team coordinator. Season 2 adds process maturity: he translates findings for the Glass Bureau and helps protect the case from overstatement. No contradiction found.

### Pixel

Pixel remains curiosity-forward and sensory. Season 1 gives him wonder and signal sensitivity; Season 2 uses him as the curious explainer and gives him the Season 3 toy-process clue. No contradiction found.

### Byte

Byte moves from Season 1 models and demos to Season 2 evidence tooling. This is a clean growth path. No contradiction found.

### Cipher

Cipher continues to seek structure. Season 2 adds a stronger role in removing false patterns and cleaning assumptions. No contradiction found.

### Grimalkin

Grimalkin's planner role becomes more visible in Season 2 through maps, alternate hypotheses, and jurisdiction paths. No contradiction found.

### Shadow

Shadow's physical observation role remains consistent. Season 2 makes small physical details part of the evidence chain. No contradiction found.

### BL4CK4T

BL4CK4T remains unseen and cryptic. Drops continue to guide method and attention. No contradiction found.

## World And Artifact Continuity

### Echo Grid And Hushline

Season 1 establishes that the Echo Grid's old issue is message-command ambiguity. The Hushline gives Signal Row a cleaner architecture. Season 2 references the Hushline model only as the place where the `-0.75` clue first appears; it does not imply the Echo Grid fix failed.

Continuity status: pass.

### Ledger Lab And Civic Learning Grid

Ledger Lab is new in Season 2 but compatible with existing Cybertropolis infrastructure. It expands the world from Signal Row into public learning systems without replacing prior districts.

Continuity status: pass.

### Threadboard

Threadboard is new in Season 2 and does not duplicate the Tonebox. Tonebox makes signal architecture visible; Threadboard orders evidence. The distinction is clear.

Continuity status: pass.

### Glass Bureau

Glass Bureau is new in Season 2. It does not contradict prior responder behavior because Season 1's problem was district-level signal confusion and Season 2's problem is cross-grid evidence ownership.

Continuity status: pass.

### Far Relay And Ledgerjack

Far Relay and Ledgerjack are draft continuity. They should remain indirect until a later public story expands them. Avoid future drafts that treat Ledgerjack as having personally caused every Season 2 event.

Continuity status: pass with watch item.

## Watch Items For Future Seasons

- Do not make every mystery originate from BL4CK4T. BL4CK4T should guide, not manufacture all risk.
- Do not make Ledgerjack the explanation for unrelated future incidents unless a later season earns that connection.
- Keep Row Rebels morally mixed if they return.
- Preserve the Hushline as a successful Season 1 outcome; future Signal Row problems should be new problems or consequences, not a simple undoing of the fix.
- Keep the Glass Bureau useful but process-heavy. Future stories should not reduce it to a joke.
- Season 3 should pick up the contained toy process and should not imply Pixel intentionally created a dangerous tool.
- Future seasons should update this audit pattern after each full draft set.
