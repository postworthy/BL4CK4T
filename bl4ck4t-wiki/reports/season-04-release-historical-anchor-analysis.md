# Season 4 Release Historical-Anchor Analysis

## Scope

This report compares the final Season 4 public release against the historical account and documentary treatment for the Mitnick/Shimomura hacker-manhunt era.

## Result

Pass.

## Historical Fidelity

- Public mythology is represented through the Notice Wall, City Chronicle, and Vanishing Caller nickname.
- Social engineering is represented through the borrowed phrase and context checks.
- Trace evidence is represented through Mirrorline Arcade, route clues, and the Chase Map.
- Media amplification is represented without turning the Chronicle into a villain.
- Proportionality is represented through the wrong poster, Glass Bureau process, and final repair path.
- The final public correction reflects the historical theme that capture or identification does not end the story.

## Transformation Integrity

- Real people are not used as fictional characters.
- Real-world technical methods are not reproduced as instructions.
- Fictional entities carry the story functions from the historical account.
- Moral complexity is preserved: the Caller causes harm, the city overreacts, and the team keeps both truths visible.

## Guardrails

- The release avoids super-hacker mythology.
- The release avoids making law or process look useless.
- The release avoids naming a culprit before evidence is ready.
- The release closes Season 4 before opening Season 5.
