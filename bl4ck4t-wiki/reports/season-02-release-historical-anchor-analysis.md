## Historical Cleanliness

The final public Season 2 files do not mention the real historical names, institutions, court case, or source-specific prosecution details. A scan of the public Season 2 files found no occurrences of the real-world names and labels that belong only in the historical account.

Transformed equivalents:

- Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory and research computing become Ledger Lab and the Civic Learning Grid.
- The seventy-five-cent accounting discrepancy becomes `-0.75`.
- Remote account misuse becomes the Borrowed Door metaphor.
- Improvised logging and tracing become the Threadboard.
- Jurisdictional response friction becomes the Glass Bureau.
- The Hanover/KGB legal layer becomes Far Relay and Ledgerjack.

## Fidelity Notes

- The release keeps the historical shape intact while avoiding real intrusion methods, real target details, and real actor names.
- The release preserves timeline discipline: the wider adversary layer does not appear until after the local record and evidence chain are established.
- The release avoids over-attribution: Ledgerjack remains a broker mark and possible identity, not a single all-powerful villain.
- The release shows false-pattern removal through the museum kiosk, which preserves the journalistic rule that a good case must rule things out.
- The release keeps the aftermath practical: better reporting, preservation, ownership, and shared evidence channels.
