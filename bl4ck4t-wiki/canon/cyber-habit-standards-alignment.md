# Cyber Habit Standards Alignment

## Summary

The BL4CK4T Field Guide habits should be friendly and story-first, but they should map to recognized cybersecurity practice. The habits are not a replacement for professional standards. They are a learner-facing bridge into the same way responsible defenders think: governance, asset awareness, safeguards, detection, response, recovery, and continuous improvement.

This page is a standing reminder for future content: public fiction should remain approachable and BL4CK4T-flavored, while classroom, product, and University layers should be able to show how the lesson aligns with current industry standards.

## Primary Standards Backbone

- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover.
- NIST SP 800-61: preparation, detection and analysis, containment/eradication/recovery, and post-incident activity.
- CISA Cybersecurity Performance Goals: practical baseline behaviors such as asset inventory, vulnerability handling, MFA, reporting, and resilience.
- CIS Controls v8: practical safeguards for inventory, configuration, access, logs, awareness, incident response, and recovery.

## BL4CK4T Seven Habits

| Field Guide habit | Standards root | BL4CK4T-world framing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Know what you protect | Govern / Identify | Name the promise before the puzzle. Know what matters, who owns it, and who could be hurt. |
| Guard the trusted paths | Protect | Keep doors, keys, updates, and message paths trustworthy before trouble arrives. |
| Watch for strange signals | Detect | Notice small changes, logs, timing, silence, repeats, and mismatches before they become a city rumor. |
| Keep evidence before story | Detect / Respond | Separate facts, guesses, unknowns, and ruled-out ideas so clues do not become accusations. |
| Report early, kindly, and clearly | Respond | Get help fast without shame, blame, or panic. The first report protects the next person. |
| Recover with consent and care | Recover | Restore known-good paths, verify repairs, preserve rollback, and do not confuse unauthorized help with safety. |
| Improve the city after the case | Govern / Recover / Training | Keep the better habit: a map, checklist, bell, warning, practice run, or clearer way to ask for help. |

## Story Use

Public fiction should teach these habits through visible civic artifacts:

- Know what you protect: Service Map, Crown Jewel Register, Device Census.
- Guard the trusted paths: Hushline, Repair Lane, verified update notices.
- Watch for strange signals: Threadboard, Status Wall, logs, notice history, timing marks.
- Keep evidence before story: Chase Map, Copy Map, confidence cards.
- Report early, kindly, and clearly: Message Office, Glass Bureau, First Bell Desk.
- Recover with consent and care: Restore Slips, Rollback Room, Consent Ledger.
- Improve the city after the case: Patch Bell, classroom prompts, case boards, new city rules.

## Voice Rule

Use the standards to keep the lessons true, but do not make public stories sound like a compliance checklist. A reader should feel the habit before they recognize the framework underneath it.

## Product And University Rule

Every future BL4CK4T University module, enterprise training artifact, classroom kit, case deck, or facilitator guide should include a standards-alignment note. The note should identify which Field Guide habit the product teaches and which standards backbone it supports. For enterprise-facing material, use buyer-legible language such as:

> Story-driven cybersecurity training aligned to recognized cybersecurity frameworks.

Use standards as the professional spine and Cybertropolis as the learner-facing skin:

- Public layer: Script Kitties, artifacts, choices, consequences, and friendly habits.
- Instructor layer: teaching objectives, discussion prompts, safe activities, and habit mapping.
- Enterprise layer: NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-61, CISA CPGs, CIS Controls, role relevance, and measurable behavior.

## Future Content Checklist

When drafting a story, product, classroom resource, or University module:

- Identify the primary Field Guide habit.
- Identify the relevant standards root.
- Teach the habit through story action before naming the standard.
- Preserve safe technical texture and defender-side behavior.
- Keep public copy friendly, not compliance-heavy.
- Make instructor or enterprise notes explicit enough for buyers, teachers, and security leaders to trust the alignment.
