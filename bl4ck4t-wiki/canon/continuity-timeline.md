# Continuity Timeline

## Summary

This timeline records the current event order from the published blog and private season drafts. Public posts are hard continuity. Wiki-only drafts are draft continuity until approved and published.

## Pilot Season - Public Hard Continuity

- 2025-09-15 - The Script Kitties receive their first known BL4CK4T drop and crack a Caesar cipher with shift five. The solved message points toward future lessons about keys.
- 2025-09-17 - BL4CK4T sends a public/private cryptography clue. The Script Kitties learn key pairs, signatures, Diffie-Hellman-style shared secrets, and the web trust lesson.
- 2025-09-18 - BL4CK4T sends the Script Kitties a compound-interest simulator. The team learns how time, rate, starting early, debt, and consistency shape outcomes.

## Season 1: The Singing Network - Public Hard Continuity

- Season 1 begins after the pilot season with Signal Row entering maintenance mode and Pixel hearing a hidden three-note phrase.
- The Script Kitties discover the Echo Grid, an old signal layer where public messages and routing instructions can blur together.
- The Little Blue Pawprint and Crunch Charm rumors spread through Packet Market and Signal Row, turning partial technical understanding into public myth.
- The Row Rebels appear as a mixed-motive Signal Row group. Rook becomes associated with curiosity, protest, status, and disruption around the Echo Grid.
- Byte's Tonebox model makes the Echo Grid visible and shows how quickly a demo can be misunderstood outside its original context.
- A false closure notice turns the Echo Grid from curiosity into public consequence.
- Mira and the Hushline establish the architectural answer: separate message paths from command paths so the city can sing without treating every song as an order.
- The season closes with a public listening exhibit and a clearer Signal Row. The Hushline meter then shows `-0.75`, and Jinx identifies it as a trail into Season 2.

## Season 2: The Seventy-Five Cent Thread - Public Hard Continuity

- Season 2 opens from the Season 1 `-0.75` cliffhanger.
- The `-0.75` appears first in the Hushline model and then in the Ledger Lab's shared resource records.
- The Ledger Lab is established as a Civic Learning Grid setting that tracks shared compute time, storage credits, lab reservations, project queues, and print resources.
- Project Orchard becomes the affected project label tied to repeated mismatches, but the draft season avoids blaming the project owners.
- The Borrowed Door becomes the defender-facing phrase for familiar access appearing in unfamiliar behavior.
- Byte and Jinx build the Threadboard as an evidence-preservation and comparison tool.
- The Glass Bureau becomes the process-heavy institution that can act once the case has clear evidence, ownership, and requested action.
- The Civic Learning Grid's old trust paths connect the Ledger Lab, a library terminal, and an old research relay.
- The Far Relay and Ledgerjack enter as an indirect broker layer connected to traded project notes and access rumors. Ledgerjack remains a mark and broker identity, not a single all-powerful villain.
- Jinx completes the season's investigator arc by presenting what was known first, what changed, what was ruled out, what remains unknown, and what the evidence supports.
- The season closes with improved anomaly reporting, a shared evidence channel, and a contained toy process that copies farther than expected, pointing toward the Morris Worm-inspired Season 3.

## Current Character Continuity

- The pilot establishes the team in a converted warehouse hideout.
- The cryptography chapter uses a rooftop setting.
- The compound-interest chapter uses a rooftop billboard and holographic simulator.
- BL4CK4T remains unseen across the current blog.
- Season 1 establishes Pixel's sensory curiosity, Cipher's structure-seeking, Byte's modeling, Shadow's physical observation, Whiskers' coordination, and Jinx's risk spotting in a team context.
- Season 2 makes Jinx the lead investigator and develops her from suspicion toward evidence discipline.
- Whiskers' leadership becomes more process-aware across Season 2.
- Byte's tools mature from models and demos toward evidence-preserving instruments.
- Grimalkin's planning role becomes explicit through maps, alternate theories, and ownership paths.
- Cipher becomes the team's strongest pattern cleaner, especially when removing false patterns.
- Shadow's small physical observations become decisive evidence links.
- Pixel remains curiosity-forward and opens the Season 3 copying-process thread.

## Open Threads

- Season 3 should pick up the toy-process cliffhanger from Season 2.
- The Row Rebels can return, but future stories should remember that Season 1 treated them as mixed-motive kids, not villains.
- The Far Relay and Ledgerjack can recur as broader adversary infrastructure, but Season 2 does not establish a captured individual mastermind.
- The Glass Bureau and shared evidence channel should remain available for later reporting and coordination stories.
- The Hushline solved the Echo Grid's message-command confusion in Signal Row, but it did not solve every system risk in Cybertropolis.
