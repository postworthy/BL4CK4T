# Mirai And The 2016 Dyn DDoS Attack: Documentary Treatment

## Purpose

Shape the historical account into a nonfiction movement structure before any fictional adaptation.

## Source Boundary

This treatment relies on ../historical-accounts/mirai-dyn-ddos.md and does not introduce fictional adaptation material.

## Documentary Thesis

Forgotten devices can still answer someone else.

## Cold Open

Open with a small concrete symptom rather than a broad claim: a waiting room, a reboot, a mismatched record, a false reading, or an ordinary device answering unexpectedly.

## Movement Structure

### Movement 1: The Symptom

- Historical function: show what people noticed first.
- Narrative question: what kind of harm is this?
- Key beats: visible symptom, early confusion, first responder uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors: chronology and source list.
- Visual/audio language: ordinary work interrupted.
- Uncertainty guardrails: avoid later certainty in early scenes.

### Movement 2: The System

- Historical function: explain the infrastructure under pressure.
- Narrative question: why did this matter?
- Key beats: dependencies, trust paths, exposed assumptions.
- Evidence anchors: technical background and infrastructure sections.
- Visual/audio language: maps, queues, records, gauges, devices.
- Uncertainty guardrails: explain mechanism without a how-to.

### Movement 3: The Response

- Historical function: show defenders acting with incomplete information.
- Narrative question: what can be done responsibly now?
- Key beats: triage, evidence preservation, public communication, repair, or containment.
- Evidence anchors: responder accounts and advisories.
- Visual/audio language: status boards and working rooms.
- Uncertainty guardrails: qualify attribution and impact claims.

### Movement 4: What Changed

- Historical function: explain lasting consequence.
- Narrative question: what did the field learn?
- Key beats: policy, practice, public imagination, unresolved debates.
- Evidence anchors: aftermath sources.
- Visual/audio language: routines that remain after the incident.
- Uncertainty guardrails: avoid overstated first-ever claims.

## Recurring Motifs

- Motif: ordinary dependence under technical pressure.
- Historical meaning: security is civic and human.
- Where it returns: opening symptom, response, aftermath.

## Interview Targets Or Archival Voices

- Original advisories, vendor posts, legal records, responders, researchers, victims, and contemporaneous reporting.

## Narration Guardrails

- Claims to state plainly: Forgotten devices can still answer someone else.
- Claims to qualify: attribution, motive, damage estimates, and scope.
- Claims to avoid: simplified hero/villain myths and operational reproduction.

## Ending

End with changed practice rather than spectacle. The historical ending should make clear why this event still teaches inventory, safer defaults, education, and manufacturer responsibility.

## Open Research Needs

- Add participant interviews or additional primary records if later revisions require more detail.
