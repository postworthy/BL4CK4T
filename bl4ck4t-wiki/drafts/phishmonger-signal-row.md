## Story Draft

The neon signs of Signal Row were brighter than usual, stacked so high above the street that their reflections shimmered across every window. Message boards clicked and bloomed. Delivery drones hummed between antenna towers. Every few seconds, another city alert blinked into the night.

Pixel loved Signal Row. "It's like the whole city is talking at once," he said, spinning beneath a billboard that painted his hoodie in blue and green light.

Jinx did not spin. Jinx watched.

Across the largest board, a black pawprint appeared.

### The Drop

The Script Kitties froze. BL4CK4T's mark pulsed once, then opened into glowing text:

> "Urgent invitation. Confirm the hideout passphrase to unlock tonight's secret lesson. Hurry before the signal expires."

Pixel's ears perked. "A secret lesson?"

Byte reached for his tablet. Cipher narrowed her eyes. Whiskers stepped closer to the sign, but Jinx held up a paw.

"Wait," Jinx said. "Since when does BL4CK4T ask us to hurry?"

The sign flickered again. A timer appeared beneath the message, counting down in sharp red numbers.

From the alley below came a silky voice. "Smart kitties act fast."

A figure in a coat of glittering message slips stepped into the light. Hooks of neon punctuation dangled from the brim of his hat.

"The Phishmonger," Jinx whispered.

### The Lesson Begins

Cipher adjusted her hood. "This is a trust problem. A message can look important and still be false."

The Phishmonger bowed. "False? Such an ugly word. I prefer exciting."

Another sign lit up behind him. Then another. Soon Signal Row was full of urgent invitations, each one wearing a pawprint, each one asking for a secret.

Whiskers read the nearest message twice. "It has the logo. It sounds like a BL4CK4T drop. But it feels wrong."

"That's the trick," Cipher said. "Phishing messages try to make you react before you think. They use urgency, prizes, fear, or authority."

Pixel bounced on his toes, then stopped himself. "So the timer is bait."

"Exactly," Jinx said. "A real lesson can wait long enough to be checked."

### Trial and Error

Byte copied one of the messages into a safe sandbox on his tablet. "No clicking. No replying. Just looking."

Pixel pointed to the pawprint. "This one is crooked."

Cipher traced the sender mark with a stylus. "And the signal path is almost right, but not quite. It says it came from BL4CK4T's drop tower, but the routing badge points to a snack kiosk."

"A very respectable snack kiosk," said The Phishmonger.

Jinx ignored him. She found another clue: the message asked for the hideout passphrase. BL4CK4T had tested their thinking before, but never asked them to give away a secret.

Whiskers nodded. "So we check the sender, check the context, inspect the clue safely, and refuse to share secrets."

"And report it," Jinx added.

Byte opened a clean city alert form and flagged the fake messages for Signal Row's wardens. Cipher wrote a short warning for nearby students:

> "If a message demands hurry and secrets, pause. Verify it another way."

The Phishmonger's smile twitched.

Pixel grinned. "What's wrong? The signal expired?"

One by one, the fake pawprints fizzled. Without panic to feed them, they shrank into harmless sparks.

### Closing Scene

The real BL4CK4T pawprint appeared above Signal Row, crisp and steady. No timer. No demand. No secret request.

> "A true signal can wait. A false one demands you hurry."

The Script Kitties stood together as the city lights settled back into their normal rhythm.

Jinx folded her arms. "I still don't trust glitter."

Cipher smiled. "You don't have to trust it. You have to verify it."

Whiskers looked across the team. "From now on: pause, check, protect, report."

Pixel tapped the final spark with one claw and watched it blink out. "And never give a passphrase to a snack kiosk."

Somewhere in the dark, The Phishmonger gathered his empty hooks and slipped away from Signal Row. But the Script Kitties had learned his favorite trick.

He could make a message urgent.

He could make it shine.

But he could not make them stop thinking.

## Teaching Tie-In

- Concept: phishing and suspicious-message handling.
- Warning signs: urgency, unexpected requests, secrets, prizes, fear, or a sender that is almost right.
- Safe response: pause, verify through another trusted path, avoid suspicious links or attachments, protect passwords and passphrases, and report the message.
- Ethics frame: cybersecurity starts with helping people make safer choices.
